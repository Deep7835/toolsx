"Flat 30% off" is easy. "30% off plus an extra 10% on the discounted price, with a ₹200 coupon above ₹999, and GST on top" is where cashiers lose money and customers lose trust. This discount and percentage calculator does the arithmetic instantly — single discounts, stacked (double) discounts, flat rupee-off, buy-more-save-more, and reverse calculations that tell you what percentage a markdown really was — so you can price festive offers, check a supplier's scheme, or settle a counter argument in seconds.

## The formulas behind every offer

- **Percentage off:** sale price = MRP × (1 − d). At 30% off ₹1,499, the price is ₹1,049.30.
- **Stacked discounts do not add.** 30% then 10% is 1 − (0.7 × 0.9) = **37%**, not 40%. On ₹1,499 that is ₹944.37, not ₹899.40 — a ₹45 difference on every unit if the cashier adds them.
- **Flat rupee off:** effective % = amount ÷ MRP. A ₹200 coupon on ₹999 is 20.02%; on ₹2,499 it is 8%.
- **Reverse:** discount % = (MRP − sale) ÷ MRP. A shirt marked ₹1,299 selling at ₹899 is 30.8% off.
- **Markup vs margin:** a 25% markup on cost is a 20% margin on selling price. Use the [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin) to keep the two straight.

## How to use it

1. Pick the mode: single discount, double discount, flat off, or "find the percentage".
2. Enter the MRP or list price and the discount figures.
3. Toggle **GST** if your price is exclusive of tax and you want the customer's final payable; pick the slab (most retail goods are 5% or 18% since GST 2.0).
4. Read the sale price, the rupee saving and the *effective* discount percentage — the number to print on the tag.
5. Copy the result to a [price tag](/tools/price-tag-generator) or a WhatsApp offer.

## Discounting and GST: what the law says

GST is charged on the **transaction value after discount** if the discount is shown on the invoice (Section 15(3)(a)). A ₹1,000 item at 20% off with 18% GST is taxed on ₹800, so the customer pays ₹944, not ₹980. Post-sale discounts — a quarter-end rebate to a dealer — reduce GST only if they were agreed before the supply and are linked to invoices via a [credit note](/tools/credit-note). Trade schemes like "buy 10 get 1 free" are treated as a discounted composite supply; tax the 11 units at the price of 10.

Retail price tags must still show the MRP under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules; you may show the offer price beside it, but you cannot sell above MRP.

## Planning festive and clearance offers

- **Know your floor.** Gross margin minus discount minus payment-gateway fee must stay positive; a 40% discount on a 45% margin product leaves 5% before rent. Check the [gross margin calculator](/tools/gross-margin).
- **Threshold offers** ("₹200 off above ₹999") lift average basket size more than flat percentages.
- **Stacked coupon abuse**: if your billing software applies a coupon after a percentage discount, make sure the tag's "effective %" matches what the customer will see.
- **Clearance math:** a 50% markdown needs double the units sold to earn the same revenue; check the [inventory turnover](/tools/inventory-turnover) before over-ordering for Diwali. ## FAQ

### Is 30% + 10% the same as 40% off?

No. The second discount applies to the already-reduced price, so the total is 37%. The calculator's double-discount mode shows both the step prices and the combined percentage.

### Should I apply GST before or after the discount?

After. GST is charged on the discounted value when the discount is shown on the invoice, which is the normal case for retail offers.

### How do I show a discount on the price tag legally?

Print the MRP, the offer price and the percentage or rupee saving. Never obscure or overprint the original MRP on the manufacturer's pack.

### What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is profit as a percentage of cost; margin is profit as a percentage of selling price. A 50% markup equals a 33.3% margin.

### Can I calculate the original price from the sale price?

Yes — choose "find the original" and enter the sale price and the discount percentage. Original = sale ÷ (1 − d).
