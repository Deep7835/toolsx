Terms and Conditions are the contract between you and everyone who uses your website, app or store: what you sell, how orders form, when you can refuse them, who owns the content, what happens when something goes wrong, and which court decides. Most small businesses copy a template from a foreign site and end up with US arbitration clauses and no mention of the Consumer Protection Act. This generator drafts **Terms & Conditions written for an Indian business** — e-commerce store, service provider, SaaS/app, marketplace seller or content site — from a short questionnaire, exported as HTML or text for your site and app-store listing.

## What the terms should cover

| Section | Why |
|---|---|
| Who you are | Legal name, address, GSTIN, contact — required for e-commerce entities under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 |
| Acceptance and eligibility | Using the site means accepting; 18+ for contracts, or parental consent |
| Products, pricing and taxes | Prices in INR inclusive of GST; right to correct errors; MRP compliance |
| Orders and payment | When a contract forms (on dispatch/confirmation, not on placing the order); payment modes; UPI/card/COD; failed payments |
| Shipping and delivery | Estimated timelines, risk passing, address accuracy — link the [refund/return policy](/tools/refund-policy) |
| Returns, refunds, cancellation | Summarised here, detailed in the refund policy; mandatory for e-commerce |
| User accounts and conduct | Accuracy of information, prohibited uses, suspension |
| Intellectual property | Your content and trademarks; user content licence — see [Trademark registration in India](/blog/trademark-registration-india-small-business-cost-process) |
| Warranties and liability | Realistic disclaimers that do not exclude what the Consumer Protection Act guarantees |
| Grievance officer | Name, contact, response time — required for e-commerce and intermediaries |
| Governing law and jurisdiction | Indian law; courts at your city; consumers retain their statutory forum rights |
| Changes | How you update terms and notify users |

## How to use the generator

1. Choose your business type and enter legal details (name, entity type, address, GSTIN, contact, grievance officer).
2. Answer the questions: do you sell goods, services, digital products or subscriptions; ship pan-India or locally; take COD; allow user accounts or reviews; run a marketplace or sell through one.
3. Set order, cancellation and refund parameters (they are echoed in the [refund policy](/tools/refund-policy)).
4. Generate, read every clause, edit anything that does not match how you actually operate.
5. Publish at `/terms`, link from the footer and checkout ("By placing this order you agree…"), and keep a dated copy of each version.

## Indian rules that shape the terms

- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and E-Commerce Rules, 2020**: mandatory disclosures (seller details, return/refund/exchange, warranty, delivery, payment methods, grievance officer, country of origin), no unfair terms, no manipulation of reviews.
- **DPDP Act**: personal data handling belongs in the [privacy policy](/tools/privacy-policy-generator); terms should reference it.
- **IT Act**: electronic contracts and click-wrap acceptance are valid; intermediaries need a grievance officer.
- **Indian Contract Act**: minors cannot contract; unconscionable clauses are void.
- **GST**: state that prices include GST where they do, and that tax invoices are issued; use the [GST invoice generator](/tools/gst-invoice).

The DPDP-specific checklist for websites and WhatsApp businesses is in [DPDP Rules 2025](/blog/dpdp-rules-2025-checklist-for-small-websites).

## FAQ

### Are Terms & Conditions mandatory?

For e-commerce entities, the E-Commerce Rules require most of these disclosures; for others they are strongly advisable because without them you have no agreed rules on orders, refunds or liability.

### Can I limit my liability completely?

No. Consumer law guarantees remedies for defective goods and deficient services; terms can limit liability for indirect losses and set reasonable procedures, not exclude statutory rights.

### Which court should I choose?

Your own city for business disputes; note that consumers can always approach the consumer commission where they live, regardless of your clause.

### Do I need separate terms for my app?

The same document can cover website and app if you include app-specific clauses (licence, updates, store terms). App stores require a link to it.

### How do I get users to accept them?

A checkbox or a clear statement at signup and checkout ("By placing this order you agree to our Terms") with a link — click-wrap acceptance is valid under the IT Act.
