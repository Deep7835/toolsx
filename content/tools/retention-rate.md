Acquiring a customer costs ₹500; keeping one costs a WhatsApp message. Retention rate measures how many customers from the start of a period are still buying at the end of it — and its mirror, churn, is the leak that no amount of marketing spend can outrun. This calculator computes **customer retention rate and churn** for any period from three numbers, shows the implied customer lifetime, and estimates what a few points of retention are worth in revenue.

## The formula

Retention rate = (Customers at end − New customers acquired during the period) ÷ Customers at start × 100.

A tiffin service started the quarter with 240 subscribers, added 60 and ended with 255: (255 − 60) ÷ 240 = **81.25%** retained; churn 18.75%. Average customer lifetime ≈ 1 ÷ churn per period — about 5.3 quarters, or 16 months — which, multiplied by monthly gross margin, gives the lifetime value that [CAC](/tools/cac-calculator) is measured against.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter customers at the start of the period, new customers acquired, and customers at the end.
2. Choose the period (month, quarter, year) — compare like with like.
3. Read the retention rate, churn rate, number lost, and the implied lifetime.
4. Enter average margin per customer per period to see the rupee value of the customers lost, and what +5 points of retention would add.
5. Track by cohort (customers who joined in the same month) to see whether newer customers stay longer.

## Defining "customer" for your business

- **Subscriptions** (tiffin, gym, broadband, SaaS): active paying accounts — clear.
- **Retail and restaurants**: a customer who bought at least once in the period; set the period to match your purchase cycle (monthly for grocery, quarterly for apparel, yearly for electronics).
- **B2B suppliers**: accounts with at least one order in the period.
- **Marketplaces**: harder, since the platform owns the customer; use repeat-order rate from your seller dashboard.

Loyalty programmes, WhatsApp order lists and a simple customer ledger in your billing software are enough to get the three numbers.

## Why a few points matter

At 80% quarterly retention a customer lasts five quarters; at 85%, nearly seven — a 33% increase in lifetime revenue from a five-point change. For a business with 500 customers at ₹1,200 quarterly margin, that is roughly ₹12 lakh of additional lifetime margin. Compare that with the cost of the retention actions below.

## FAQ

### What is a good retention rate?

Subscription businesses aim for 90%+ monthly; retail repeat rates of 30–40% quarterly are healthy; B2B suppliers 80–90% annually. Compare with your own history first.

### Why subtract new customers?

Because the formula measures how many of the *original* customers stayed. Counting new ones would hide churn behind acquisition.

### How is churn different from retention?

Churn = 100% − retention. Both describe the same movement; churn is the share lost.

### Can retention exceed 100%?

No. If your inputs produce more than 100%, new customers were undercounted or the end figure includes reactivated old customers — decide how to treat those and stay consistent.

### How do I measure retention without software?

A spreadsheet with customer phone numbers and last order date is enough: count active in period A, active in period B, and new in B.
