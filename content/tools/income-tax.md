Old regime or new? For most salaried and small-business taxpayers it is the only tax decision that matters, and it should be made with numbers, not habit. This calculator computes your liability for **FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28)** under both regimes side by side — slab tax, rebate under Section 87A, standard deduction, surcharge with marginal relief and 4% cess — and tells you the break-even level of deductions at which the old regime starts to win. It runs entirely in your browser.

## The FY 2026-27 slabs

**New regime (default):**

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4 lakh | Nil |
| ₹4–8 lakh | 5% |
| ₹8–12 lakh | 10% |
| ₹12–16 lakh | 15% |
| ₹16–20 lakh | 20% |
| ₹20–24 lakh | 25% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% |

Rebate under 87A wipes out tax for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (maximum rebate ₹60,000), with marginal relief just above it. Standard deduction for salaried taxpayers is ₹75,000, so a salary of ₹12.75 lakh is tax-free. Employer NPS contribution (up to 14% of basic) is deductible; most other deductions are not.

**Old regime:** basic exemption ₹2.5 lakh (₹3 lakh for 60+, ₹5 lakh for 80+), then 5% to ₹5 lakh, 20% to ₹10 lakh, 30% above; rebate of ₹12,500 up to ₹5 lakh; standard deduction ₹50,000; plus 80C (₹1.5 lakh), 80D, HRA, home-loan interest, 80CCD(1B) and the rest.

Both regimes add 4% health and education cess, and surcharge from ₹50 lakh (10%), ₹1 crore (15%), ₹2 crore (25%) and — old regime only — ₹5 crore (37%). The Income Tax Act 2025 that took effect on 1 April 2026 renumbers sections but keeps these rates; what it does change is covered in [Income Tax Act 2025: what actually changes](/blog/income-tax-act-2025-what-changes-from-april-2026).

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter salary (gross, before deductions) or business/professional income, plus other income such as interest or rent.
2. Fill in the deductions you actually claim — 80C, 80D, HRA exemption (compute it with the [HRA exemption calculator](/tools/hra-exemption)), home-loan interest, NPS, professional tax.
3. Set your age band for the old-regime exemption limit.
4. Read both liabilities, the difference, and the **break-even deductions** figure — the total deductions you need for the old regime to be cheaper.
5. If you are salaried, tell payroll your choice in April; business taxpayers who opt out of the new regime must file Form 10-IEA and can switch back only once.

## Who should still consider the old regime

The new regime wins for most people with deductions below roughly ₹4–4.5 lakh at higher incomes, and for almost everyone up to ₹12.75 lakh salary. The old regime can still win if you stack a large HRA exemption in a metro, ₹2 lakh home-loan interest, ₹1.5 lakh 80C, ₹50,000 NPS and ₹25,000–75,000 of health premiums. ## Business and freelance income

Freelancers under **44ADA** declare 50% of gross receipts (up to ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are under 5%) as income; small businesses under **44AD** declare 6–8% of turnover (up to ₹3 crore). Enter that presumptive income here as business income. Remember advance tax: presumptive taxpayers pay 100% by 15 March, others in four instalments — use the [advance tax calculator](/tools/advance-tax). ## FAQ

### Is income up to ₹12 lakh really tax-free under the new regime?

Yes, for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh the 87A rebate cancels the tax. Salaried taxpayers also get the ₹75,000 standard deduction, so gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh is tax-free. Special-rate income (capital gains) does not get the rebate.

### Can I switch regimes every year?

Salaried taxpayers without business income can choose each year at filing. Those with business or professional income can opt out of the new regime once and return to it only once.

### Does the new regime allow any deductions?

Standard deduction of ₹75,000 for salaried, employer NPS contribution under 80CCD(2), family pension deduction, and a few others. 80C, 80D, HRA and home-loan interest on self-occupied property are not allowed.

### What is marginal relief?

If a small increase in income above ₹12 lakh (new regime) or above surcharge thresholds would raise tax by more than the extra income, the tax is limited to that extra income. The calculator applies it automatically.

### Are the slabs different for senior citizens?

Under the new regime, no. Under the old regime the basic exemption is ₹3 lakh for those aged 60–79 and ₹5 lakh for 80 and above.
