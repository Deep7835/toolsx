A tailoring unit with six machines that runs them 60% of the time, a café with 30 seats that fills 45% of them on average, a printing press running one shift when it could run two — each is paying full fixed costs for partial output. Capacity utilisation is actual output as a percentage of the maximum you could produce with what you already have, and it decides whether the next order needs a new machine or just a second shift. This calculator computes **utilisation** for machines, seats, hours or units, shows the idle capacity in units and rupees, and finds the break-even utilisation for your fixed costs.

## The formula

Capacity utilisation = Actual output ÷ Maximum possible output × 100.

Six machines × 8 hours × 26 days = 1,248 machine-hours a month; if 750 were productive, utilisation is **60%**. At a contribution of ₹180 per machine-hour, the idle 498 hours are ₹89,640 of contribution not earned. Define "maximum" realistically — practical capacity after maintenance, breaks and changeovers, usually 80–85% of theoretical.

## How to use the calculator

1. Choose the unit of capacity: machine-hours, labour-hours, seats × sessions, units per day, or cubic metres of storage.
2. Enter the maximum (or the pieces: number of machines/seats, hours, days) and the actual output for the period.
3. Enter contribution per unit (from the [contribution margin calculator](/tools/contribution-margin)) to see the value of idle capacity.
4. Enter fixed costs to see the **break-even utilisation** — the level at which the business covers its fixed costs.
5. Track by shift, day of week or machine; averages hide a full morning and an empty evening.

## Reading the number

- **Below 60%**: fixed costs are the problem; fill capacity before cutting price — a bulk order at a discount that still covers variable cost adds contribution (see [contribution margin](/tools/contribution-margin)).
- **60–85%**: healthy; room for growth without capex.
- **Above 85–90%**: queues, overtime and quality slips appear; plan capacity — a second shift, a machine, or outsourcing — before the [defect rate](/tools/defect-rate) rises and [on-time delivery](/tools/on-time-delivery) falls.
- Compare with demand: high utilisation and a growing order book justify capex; check the return with the [ROI calculator](/tools/roi-calculator) and the financing with the [EMI calculator](/tools/emi-calculator).

## Raising utilisation without capex

- **Off-peak pricing**: cafés, salons, gyms and clinics can fill 3–5 pm with a 15% discount that still beats an empty seat; the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) shows the margin.
- **Second shift or extended hours** — often cheaper per unit than a new machine, but check overtime rules and the [team payroll](/tools/team-payroll) cost.
- **Job work for others** during slack weeks; goods move on a [delivery challan](/tools/delivery-challan).
- **Reduce changeover time** and batch similar jobs.
- **Take marketplace or ONDC orders** to smooth demand; see [Selling on ONDC in 2026](/blog/ondc-seller-guide-2026).

## Utilisation, cost and price

Unit cost falls as utilisation rises because fixed cost spreads over more units: at 60% utilisation the unit's share of ₹3 lakh monthly fixed costs is ₹400; at 80%, ₹300. That is why quoting from full-cost at low utilisation loses orders — quote from contribution and target utilisation, and revisit the [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin) quarterly.

## FAQ

### What is a good capacity utilisation rate?

70–85% for most manufacturing and services; restaurants and salons often run 50–65% overall with full peaks. Above 90% sustained usually means you are losing orders or quality.

### Theoretical or practical capacity?

Practical — after maintenance, breaks, changeovers and realistic staffing. Theoretical capacity makes every utilisation figure look worse than it is.

### How do I measure capacity for a service business?

Available hours × staff (or seats × sessions) as the maximum, and billed or occupied hours as actual. A salon with 4 chairs × 10 hours × 26 days has 1,040 chair-hours.

### When should I add capacity?

When utilisation stays above 85% with a growing order book and the incremental contribution from new capacity covers its EMI and fixed costs within a reasonable payback.

### Does utilisation include rework?

No. Count only good output. Rework hours are lost capacity and show up in the defect rate.
