₹5,000 a month feels small; ₹5,000 a month for 20 years at 12% is ₹49.96 lakh, of which only ₹12 lakh was your money. That is the whole argument for a Systematic Investment Plan, and this calculator lets you see it with your own numbers: monthly SIP or a lumpsum, expected return, tenure, an annual **step-up** to match salary increases, and inflation-adjusted value so the figure is honest. It also runs the reverse question — what monthly SIP reaches a target — and compares SIP with lumpsum for the same total outlay.

## The maths

Future value of a SIP = A × ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1) ÷ r × (1 + r), where A is the monthly amount, r the monthly return, n the months (assuming investment at the start of each month). For a lumpsum, FV = P × (1 + R)ᵗ. A step-up SIP raises A by a fixed percentage each year — a 10% annual step-up on ₹5,000 for 20 years at 12% ends near ₹1 crore instead of ₹50 lakh. The three variables that decide the outcome — amount, time and return — are compared in [SIP explained: how ₹5,000 a month becomes ₹50 lakh](/blog/sip-how-5000-a-month-grows).

## How to use the calculator

1. Choose **SIP**, **lumpsum** or **goal** mode.
2. Enter the monthly amount (or target corpus), the expected annual return and the tenure in years. 10–12% is a common assumption for diversified equity funds over long periods; 6–7% for debt.
3. Add an annual step-up percentage if you plan to increase the SIP each year.
4. Toggle inflation (say 5–6%) to see the corpus in today's rupees.
5. Read the invested amount, the estimated returns, the final value and the year-by-year chart; download the schedule as CSV.

## Returns, tax and the new regime

- **Equity funds**: gains on units held over a year are long-term, taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year; short-term gains at 20%.
- **Debt funds** bought after April 2023 are taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period.
- **ELSS** gives 80C deduction with a 3-year lock-in — but that deduction only helps under the old regime. If your tax is already nil under the new regime, choose funds on merit, not on 80C; the trade-off is worked through in [₹12 lakh tax-free: should you still invest in 80C?](/blog/new-tax-regime-12-lakh-tax-free-should-you-still-invest-80c) and the regime itself in the [income tax calculator](/tools/income-tax).
- Every SIP instalment is a separate purchase with its own holding period; redeem older units first for the lower rate.

## For business owners

A SIP is the simplest way to turn irregular business income into a retirement corpus that the business cannot eat. Use a business-cycle-aware amount (lower in lean months, step-up after the festive quarter), keep it in a personal account, and treat it like an EMI to yourself — the [EMI calculator](/tools/emi-calculator) shows the mirror image of the same compounding. Compare the outcome with your [EPF projection](/tools/epf-gratuity) if you are salaried; both together are the plan.

## Assumptions to be careful with

- Returns are not linear; a 12% average includes years of −20% and +40%. Longer tenures smooth this.
- Expense ratios and exit loads reduce returns; direct plans save 0.5–1% a year.
- Step-up SIPs assume you will actually increase the amount; automate it.
- Inflation of 5–6% halves purchasing power in about 12–14 years — look at the real value, not just the nominal one.

## FAQ

### What return should I assume for a SIP?

Long-run diversified equity funds in India have delivered roughly 11–14% annualised; using 10–12% is prudent. Debt funds return about 6–7.5%. The calculator lets you test a range.

### What is a step-up SIP?

A SIP whose amount increases by a set percentage every year, typically matching salary growth. It dramatically raises the final corpus without a large starting commitment.

### Is SIP better than lumpsum?

A lumpsum invested early outperforms in a rising market; a SIP reduces timing risk and suits monthly income. The comparison mode shows both for the same total investment.

### Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. They depend on the fund's performance. Only the maths is certain; the return input is an assumption.

### How is SIP income taxed?

Equity fund gains held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% beyond ₹1.25 lakh per year; shorter holdings at 20%. Debt fund gains are added to income and taxed at slab rates.
