The customer who messages three shops buys from the one that replies first — not the cheapest, not the best-rated, the first. First response time is the interval between a customer's first message, call or email and your first meaningful reply. It is the single most controllable driver of lead conversion for a small business, and it is usually measured in hours when it should be minutes. This calculator averages your response times across WhatsApp, calls, email and Instagram DMs, shows the median and the worst cases, and compares business-hours performance with after-hours.

## The formula

Average first response time = Sum of (first reply time − first message time) ÷ Number of conversations.

Report the **median** alongside the average — one enquiry left unanswered over a weekend can turn a 12-minute average into 3 hours. Track **within business hours** separately: a message at 11 pm answered at 9 am is a 10-hour response by the clock but a 0-minute response by your service promise, provided the promise is stated.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter response times for the period — paste a list (in minutes), or enter per-channel totals and counts.
2. Read the average, median, 90th percentile and the share answered within your target (say 15 minutes).
3. Split by channel and by business hours vs after hours.
4. Set a target and see how many conversations missed it; those are your lost leads.
5. Track weekly; combine with the [lead conversion rate](/tools/lead-conversion) to see the effect on sales.

## Why minutes matter

Studies of lead response across industries consistently find that replies within five minutes convert many times better than replies after an hour, and that the drop-off continues through the day. For a shop with 40 WhatsApp enquiries a day at 15% conversion, cutting the median response from 45 minutes to 5 can plausibly add 3–5 orders a day. That is worth more than most ad budgets — the [CAC calculator](/tools/cac-calculator) will show the equivalent spend.

## Getting to a 5-minute median

- **WhatsApp Business greeting and away messages** — an instant acknowledgement with your hours and a link to the [menu](/tools/menu-creator) or catalogue buys you time without losing the lead.
- **Quick replies** for the ten most common questions (price, delivery area, timing, GST invoice).
- **One person owns the inbox** per shift; rotate, but never leave it ownerless.
- **Notifications on** for the business number; a separate phone for the shop line.
- **Calls**: miss fewer with a call-back rule — every missed call gets a WhatsApp within 10 minutes.
- **After hours**: state your hours in the away message; customers accept a next-morning reply if they were told.
- Automation must respect consent rules — see [TRAI's new anti-spam rules](/blog/trai-anti-spam-rules-2026-businesses-that-call-or-message-customers) and [WhatsApp Business pricing](/blog/whatsapp-business-per-message-pricing-2026).

## Response time and the other support metrics

Fast replies that do not solve the problem generate repeat contacts — track [first contact resolution](/tools/fcr-rate) with it. Satisfaction ([CSAT](/tools/csat-calculator)) usually rises with speed until about the 15-minute mark and then plateaus; beyond that, resolution and tone matter more.

## FAQ

### What is a good first response time?

Under 15 minutes during business hours for WhatsApp and calls; under 2 hours for email. Under 5 minutes is where conversion gains are largest.

### Does an automated greeting count as a response?

No. Count the first human (or genuinely helpful) reply. Auto-greetings help retention of the lead but should not be reported as response time.

### How do I measure it on WhatsApp?

WhatsApp Business shows message timestamps; sample 30–50 conversations a week and note the gap. Business API providers report it automatically.

### Should I measure after-hours messages?

Yes, separately. Report business-hours response time as your service level, and after-hours as "answered by [time next morning]".

### What is the 90th percentile and why show it?

The time within which 90% of conversations were answered. It exposes the slow tail that averages hide — usually weekends and lunch breaks.
