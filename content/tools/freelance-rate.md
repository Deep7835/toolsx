"₹500 an hour" sounds like a lot until you count the hours nobody pays for — pitching, invoicing, learning, waiting for approvals — and the costs nobody sees: software, the laptop, the accountant, GST filings, health insurance, the months with no work. This calculator works backwards from the **income you want** to the **hourly, daily and project rates you must charge**, using your real billable hours, business costs, tax and a buffer for slow months — so a quote is a number you can defend rather than a guess.

## The arithmetic

1. **Target take-home** (say ₹12 lakh a year) + **business costs** (software, hardware, internet, accountant, insurance: ₹1.5 lakh) + **income tax** (estimated with the [income tax calculator](/tools/income-tax); under 44ADA you are taxed on 50% of receipts) = **required gross** ≈ ₹14.5 lakh.
2. **Billable hours** = working days (say 230) × hours a day (8) × utilisation (60% — the share of time actually billed) ≈ 1,100 hours.
3. **Hourly rate** = 14.5 lakh ÷ 1,100 ≈ **₹1,320**; daily rate ≈ ₹10,500; a 40-hour project ≈ ₹53,000 — before GST.

Utilisation is the number most freelancers overestimate; 50–65% is realistic once admin, sales and gaps are counted.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter the annual take-home you want and your business costs (the tool suggests typical items).
2. Enter working days, hours per day and expected utilisation; add leave and a buffer for lean months.
3. Choose the tax basis — 44ADA presumptive (50% of receipts taxed) or actual expenses — and the regime.
4. Read the hourly, daily and weekly rates, and the project rate for any number of hours.
5. Compare with the loaded cost of an employee doing the same work ([employee cost calculator](/tools/employee-cost)) — clients think in that number.

## GST for freelancers

Registration is compulsory once receipts cross **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in special-category states) or if you provide services across state lines through platforms that require it. Registered freelancers charge 18% on top of the rate for domestic clients (B2B clients claim it back, so quote exclusive of GST), and invoice exports at zero rate under a Letter of Undertaking. Presumptive tax under 44ADA, quarterly advance tax and which ITR to file are covered in [Freelancer taxes in India: 44ADA, GST, LUT](/blog/freelancer-taxes-india-44ada-gst) and the [advance tax calculator](/tools/advance-tax).

## Pricing beyond the hour

- **Project and value pricing** beat hourly for defined outcomes; use the hourly rate as your floor, then price the result.
- **Retainers** smooth cash flow — a monthly fee for a block of hours at a small discount.
- **Rush and scope-change premiums**: 25–50% for deadlines under 48 hours; every change gets a revised [service quote](/tools/service-quote).
- **Foreign clients**: quote in USD or EUR with a 2–3% conversion buffer; check rates in the [currency converter](/tools/currency-converter).
- **Raise rates annually** — 8–10% keeps pace with costs and signals demand; grandfather good clients for a quarter.

## Getting paid

- Advance of 30–50% on a [proforma invoice](/tools/proforma-invoice); balance on delivery via a [GST invoice](/tools/gst-invoice) with a UPI QR or bank details.
- Payment terms in writing; for registered MSMEs (Udyam covers freelancers as micro enterprises), the 45-day rule and interest apply — see [Section 43B(h)](/blog/section-43b-h-45-day-payment-rule) and the [late payment interest calculator](/tools/late-payment-interest).
- Clients deduct 10% TDS under 194J on professional fees above ₹50,000 a year; claim it against your tax.

## FAQ

### What utilisation should I assume?

50–65%. Fully booked months are balanced by pitching, admin and gaps; assuming 100% sets a rate that leaves you short.

### Should I quote hourly or per project?

Use hourly to compute your floor; quote per project or per outcome wherever the scope is clear. Clients prefer certainty and you keep the upside of working fast.

### Do I add GST on top of the rate?

If registered, yes — 18% on domestic services, shown separately; B2B clients recover it. Exports are zero-rated with an LUT.

### How does 44ADA affect my rate?

Under 44ADA, half your receipts are deemed profit and taxed; the calculator applies that to estimate the tax component of your required gross.

### How often should I raise rates?

Once a year, by 8–10%, with a month's notice to retained clients; more for new clients when demand exceeds your hours.
