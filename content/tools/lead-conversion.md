Forty WhatsApp enquiries a day and six orders is a 15% conversion rate — and a business that could double sales without spending another rupee on ads by fixing how it answers the other 34. Lead conversion rate is the share of enquiries (calls, chats, form fills, walk-ins, quotes sent) that become paying customers. This calculator computes it for any period and channel, shows the revenue impact of each percentage point, and works backwards: how many leads you need for a sales target at your current rate.

## The formula

Conversion rate = Customers won ÷ Leads received × 100.

Track it at the stage that matters for you — enquiry → quote, quote → order, walk-in → sale — and by channel: Instagram DMs, Google Business calls, JustDial, referrals, marketplace queries. A furniture maker converting 40% of referrals and 8% of JustDial leads should spend accordingly. For multi-step pipelines, the [marketing funnel calculator](/tools/marketing-funnel) breaks the journey into stages.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter leads received and customers won for the period (and per channel, if you have it).
2. Enter average order value to see revenue per lead and the value of +1 percentage point.
3. Use **target mode**: enter the sales target and the tool tells you the leads needed at the current rate — or the rate needed from the leads you have.
4. Track monthly; a falling rate with steady lead volume points to response time, pricing or competition, not marketing.

## What moves conversion for small businesses

- **Speed**: leads answered within 5 minutes convert several times better than those answered after an hour. Measure with the [first response time](/tools/first-response-time) tool; a WhatsApp quick-reply template helps.
- **A written quote within the hour**: the [service quote builder](/tools/service-quote) produces one in minutes; enquiries that get a PDF quote convert better than "call me for price".
- **Proof**: reviews, photos of past work, a GST invoice promise. The [Google review request builder](/tools/google-review) keeps the proof fresh.
- **Follow-up**: most lost leads were never followed up twice. A second message after 24 hours and a third after a week recovers 10–20%.
- **Qualification**: a form or a first question that filters budget and location raises the rate by removing leads that were never going to buy — track the [win rate](/tools/win-rate) on qualified opportunities separately.
- **Price clarity**: "starting at ₹4,500" beats "DM for price".

## Interpreting the number with cost

A 15% rate on ₹100 leads is a [CAC](/tools/cac-calculator) of ₹667; raising conversion to 20% cuts it to ₹500 without touching ad spend. That is why fixing conversion should come before buying more traffic — and why the [ROAS](/tools/roas-calculator) of the same campaign moves with it.

## FAQ

### What counts as a lead?

Any identifiable enquiry with intent: a call, a chat, a form, a quote request, a walk-in who asks about a product. Define it once and keep the definition constant so trends are meaningful.

### What is a good lead conversion rate?

Walk-in retail 20–40%, WhatsApp and local services 15–30%, web forms 5–15%. Your own trend by channel matters more than benchmarks.

### Should I count repeat customers?

No — measure new leads to new customers for conversion; repeat business belongs in [retention](/tools/retention-rate).

### How do I track leads without a CRM?

A spreadsheet with date, source, name, status and value is enough. Update it daily; the calculator takes the totals.

### How quickly should I respond to a lead?

Within minutes during business hours. Conversion falls sharply after the first hour, and most competitors respond slowly, so speed alone is an advantage.
