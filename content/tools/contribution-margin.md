Every sale has to do two jobs: pay for itself, and pay a little towards the rent. Contribution margin measures the second job — selling price minus all **variable** costs per unit — and it is the number behind every break-even, every "should we take this order at a discount", and every "how many more do we need to sell to cover the new salesperson". This calculator computes contribution per unit, contribution margin ratio, break-even units and revenue, and the margin of safety, and lets you test price and cost changes instantly.

## The formulas

- **Contribution per unit** = Price − Variable cost per unit.
- **Contribution margin ratio** = Contribution ÷ Price.
- **Break-even units** = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution per unit.
- **Break-even revenue** = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin ratio.
- **Margin of safety** = (Actual sales − Break-even sales) ÷ Actual sales.

A café sells a coffee at ₹150; milk, beans, cup and lid cost ₹45 and the UPI/card fee ₹2 → contribution ₹103 (68.7%). With ₹1,50,000 of monthly fixed costs, break-even is 1,457 coffees a month, about 49 a day. Selling 2,000 a month gives a margin of safety of 27%.

## Variable vs fixed

| Variable (per unit or per sale) | Fixed (per month) |
|---|---|
| Materials, purchase cost, packaging | Rent, salaries of permanent staff |
| Payment gateway or MDR fees | Electricity base charge, internet, software |
| Marketplace commission and shipping | Loan EMIs, insurance |
| Sales commission per order | Depreciation |
| Delivery fuel per trip ([fuel expense calculator](/tools/fuel-expense)) | Marketing retainers |

Semi-variable costs (electricity for machines, overtime) can be split; when in doubt, treat the portion that rises with volume as variable.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter the selling price (excluding GST) and each variable cost per unit — or a variable-cost percentage for fees and commissions.
2. Enter monthly fixed costs.
3. Read contribution per unit, the ratio, break-even units and revenue.
4. Enter current or expected monthly sales to see profit and the margin of safety.
5. Try scenarios: a ₹10 price cut, a cheaper supplier, a new fixed cost like a hire (use the loaded figure from the [employee cost calculator](/tools/employee-cost)).

## Decisions it answers

- **Accept a bulk order at a discount?** Yes if the discounted price still exceeds variable cost and you have spare capacity — it adds contribution even below full-cost price. Check spare capacity with the [capacity utilization calculator](/tools/capacity-utilization).
- **Which product to push?** The one with the highest contribution per unit of the scarce resource (shelf space, machine hour, staff hour), not the highest price.
- **Drop a product?** Only if its contribution is negative or the capacity has a better use — a low-margin product still covering some fixed cost is worth keeping until replaced.
- **Raise price or cut cost?** A ₹10 price rise adds ₹10 contribution; a ₹10 cost cut adds the same but is often harder — test both here, then run the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) for the customer-facing view.
- **Marketplace vs own channel**: commission and shipping are variable; the [seller pricing workflow](/tools/seller-pricing) shows the contribution per channel.

## Multi-product businesses

Compute a weighted contribution margin ratio across your sales mix (each product's ratio × its share of revenue) and divide fixed costs by it for overall break-even revenue. A shift in mix towards high-contribution items lowers break-even without any price change — the quiet lever behind "add-on" selling in cafés and accessory racks in electronics stores.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between contribution margin and gross margin?

Gross margin subtracts only cost of goods; contribution margin subtracts all variable costs, including payment fees, commissions and per-order shipping. Contribution is the right basis for break-even.

### Can contribution margin be negative?

Yes, when variable cost exceeds price — every sale then loses money. Fix price or cost before selling more.

### How do I treat GST?

Exclude it. Use tax-exclusive price and costs (net of input credit).

### What is a good contribution margin ratio?

Retail 20–40%, food service 60–70%, services 60–80%. What matters is whether volume × contribution covers fixed costs with room to spare.

### How does a discount affect break-even?

A 10% discount on a 30% contribution ratio cuts contribution by a third, raising break-even units by 50%. The calculator shows the new figure instantly.
