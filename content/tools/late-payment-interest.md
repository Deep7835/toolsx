A customer who pays 90 days late is borrowing from you at zero interest — unless you charge it. Indian law gives micro and small enterprises an unusually strong lever: under the **MSMED Act**, a buyer who pays a registered MSME supplier after the agreed period (maximum 45 days) owes **compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate**, and cannot deduct the interest as a business expense. This calculator computes that interest, or simple interest at your contract rate, for any invoice and any delay, so your reminder letter carries an exact figure.

## The MSMED Act formula

- **Due date**: the date agreed in writing, capped at **45 days** from acceptance (or deemed acceptance) of goods or services; **15 days** if there is no written agreement.
- **Rate**: three times the RBI bank rate, currently 5.5% → **16.5% per annum**, compounded **monthly**.
- **Who can claim**: suppliers registered as micro or small enterprises on Udyam at the time of supply; medium enterprises are not covered.
- **Buyer's side**: the interest is not deductible for income tax, and Section 43B(h) also disallows the *principal* expense until it is actually paid if the payment is late. Companies must disclose outstanding MSME dues in their accounts.

Recovery goes through the MSME Samadhaan portal to the state Facilitation Council, which can order payment with interest; the process and the new deemed-acceptance rules are explained in [Section 43B(h): the 45-day payment rule](/blog/section-43b-h-45-day-payment-rule) and [The new MSME definition: 7 benefits](/blog/new-msme-definition-udyam-2025-benefits).

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter the invoice (or overdue) amount.
2. Enter the due date — the agreed date, or 45 days from delivery — and the payment date (or today).
3. Choose **MSMED Act** (3× bank rate, monthly compounding; edit the bank rate if it changes) or **contract rate** (simple interest at the rate on your invoice or agreement, e.g. 18% or 24%).
4. Read the days overdue, the interest, and the total payable; copy the breakdown into your reminder or a [debit note](/tools/debit-note).
5. For several invoices, run each and total — or use the CSV export.

## Worked example

Invoice ₹2,50,000, due 1 July 2026, paid 20 September 2026 (81 days late). MSMED interest at 16.5% compounded monthly ≈ ₹9,350. At an 18% simple contract rate: 2,50,000 × 18% × 81 ÷ 365 ≈ ₹9,990. Either way, roughly ₹10,000 the buyer was silently borrowing.

## Charging interest in practice

- **Print the term** on every [GST invoice](/tools/gst-invoice) and [purchase order](/tools/purchase-order): "Interest at 18% p.a. on overdue amounts; MSMED Act applies."
- **GST on interest**: interest for delayed payment is part of the value of supply and attracts GST at the rate of the underlying goods or services, payable when received; raise a [debit note](/tools/debit-note) for it.
- **Section 138 cheques**: if the customer's cheque bounced, the notice-and-complaint clock runs in parallel; see [Cheque bounce: what to do](/blog/cheque-bounce-section-138-what-to-do).
- **Big buyers** now watch 43B(h) closely; a polite reminder citing the section moves invoices up the queue.
- Count the days with the [business days calculator](/tools/business-days) when the contract specifies working days — the Act itself uses calendar days.

## Other uses

- **GST and income tax dues**: interest is 18% simple (GST) or 1% per month (income tax); use contract mode.
- **Loan or rent arrears**: enter the agreed rate.
- **Society maintenance arrears**: most bye-laws allow up to 21% simple; the [society maintenance invoice](/tools/society-maintenance) tool applies it.

## FAQ

### What interest rate applies under the MSMED Act?

Three times the RBI bank rate, compounded monthly — 16.5% a year at the current 5.5% bank rate.

### Can a buyer and supplier agree on a longer credit period than 45 days?

No. Any written agreement is capped at 45 days from acceptance; a longer period is read down to 45 days.

### Do I need to be registered to claim?

Yes — Udyam registration as a micro or small enterprise on the date of supply. Medium enterprises cannot claim under the Act.

### Is GST payable on late-payment interest?

Yes. Interest for delayed payment is included in the value of supply and taxed at the rate of the goods or services, when actually received.

### How do I recover the money?

File on MSME Samadhaan; the Facilitation Council conciliates and, failing that, arbitrates. Awards typically include the statutory interest.
