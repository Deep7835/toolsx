"Delivery charges ₹60" is either a bargain for the customer or a slow leak for you — and most delivery businesses never check which. Fuel is the largest variable cost of every trip, but tolls, parking, a driver's time and wear add up too. This calculator turns a trip (or a day's route) into a **cost per trip and per kilometre** from distance, mileage and today's fuel price, adds other costs, applies your markup, and shows what to charge — for two-wheeler deliveries, tempo runs, field service visits and client travel that you bill back.

## The arithmetic

Fuel cost = Distance ÷ Mileage × Fuel price. A 38 km round trip on a scooter doing 45 km/l at ₹96 a litre costs ₹81 in petrol. Add ₹0 tolls and a ₹20 share of the day's parking → ₹101 direct cost. At a 20% markup, bill ₹121; per km, about ₹3.20. A Tata Ace doing 12 km/l on diesel at ₹89 costs ₹7.40 per km in fuel alone before the driver.

Markup covers what the trip-level numbers miss: the driver's salary, insurance, servicing, tyres and the vehicle's depreciation. A realistic full cost for a two-wheeler is ₹5–7 per km; for a small commercial vehicle ₹15–25 per km.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter the round-trip distance (or pick it from Maps) and the vehicle's real mileage — from your own fill-to-fill record, not the brochure.
2. Choose fuel type (petrol, diesel, CNG per kg) and today's price.
3. Add tolls, parking, driver payment and any per-trip item.
4. Set the customer markup, or switch to "cost only" for internal costing.
5. Read cost, price to bill and per-km figures; use the **route mode** to total a day of stops.

## Delivery pricing that works

- **Zone pricing**: free within 2 km, ₹40 for 2–5 km, ₹80 beyond — simpler than per-km and easier to print on the [digital menu](/tools/menu-creator).
- **Minimum order for free delivery** set from your [contribution margin](/tools/contribution-margin) — the delivery cost must fit inside it.
- **Batch deliveries** — three drops on one 12 km loop cost a third each of what three separate trips do.
- **Marketplace comparison**: a ₹90 platform delivery fee versus your own ₹45 real cost is one reason direct orders on a [WhatsApp link](/tools/whatsapp-direct) win. The trade-offs are in [Quick commerce vs the kirana](/blog/quick-commerce-vs-kirana-how-to-compete).

## Billing travel to clients

Service businesses (repair, installation, consulting) bill travel as a line on the [service quote](/tools/service-quote) or [GST invoice](/tools/gst-invoice). Travel billed as part of the service takes the service's GST rate — it is not a separate exempt "reimbursement" unless you act as a pure agent with actual bills. Keep a trip log with date, client, distance and purpose; it supports both the invoice and the vehicle expense claim in your books.

## Tax and records

- Fuel, maintenance and insurance for vehicles used in business are deductible; personal use must be excluded.
- Depreciation on delivery vehicles is 15% (30% if run on hire) under income tax — see the [depreciation calculator](/tools/depreciation-calculator).
- GST on fuel is not creditable (fuel is outside GST); ITC on vehicle purchase is blocked for most businesses except transport and goods vehicles.
- Cash fuel payments above ₹10,000 a day per pump are disallowed under Section 40A(3); pay by card or UPI.

## FAQ

### What mileage should I use?

Your measured mileage — fill the tank, note the odometer, fill again after a week, divide. City delivery mileage is typically 20–30% below the brochure figure.

### How do I include the driver's cost?

Enter the driver's pay for the trip (or per-day pay ÷ trips) in the "other costs" field, or fold it into the markup for a rougher estimate.

### Should I charge GST on delivery?

If you charge delivery with a taxable product or service, it is part of the supply and takes the same GST rate. Composition dealers cannot charge GST separately.

### Is CNG cheaper than petrol per km?

Usually 40–50% cheaper per km at current prices, though CNG vehicles carry extra cost and refuelling time. Enter CNG price per kg and the vehicle's km/kg to compare.

### Can I use this for employee travel reimbursement?

Yes — set markup to zero and use the per-km cost, or use the government-style flat rates your policy specifies.
