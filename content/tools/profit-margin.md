Most small businesses know their sales and their bank balance and guess everything in between. The profit margin and break-even calculator replaces the guess: enter cost price, selling price, packaging, payment-gateway or UPI fees, and fixed monthly costs, and it returns **gross margin, net margin, markup, and the number of units you must sell each month to break even** — the four numbers that decide whether a product is worth stocking and a price is worth quoting.

## Margin vs markup

- **Markup** is profit as a percentage of cost: buy at ₹200, sell at ₹300 → markup 50%.
- **Gross margin** is profit as a percentage of selling price: the same sale is a 33.3% margin.
- **Net margin** subtracts variable per-unit costs (packaging, gateway fee, shipping subsidy) and the unit's share of fixed costs.

Confusing markup with margin is the classic pricing error — a "40% margin" applied as a 40% markup on cost actually yields 28.6%. Both are shown side by side here; the [gross margin calculator](/tools/gross-margin) and [net profit margin calculator](/tools/net-profit-margin) go deeper on each.

## Break-even

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit, where contribution = selling price − variable cost per unit. A stall with ₹30,000 monthly rent and staff, selling samosas at ₹20 with ₹9 of ingredients and packaging, needs 30,000 ÷ 11 = **2,728 samosas a month** — about 91 a day — before it earns a rupee. The [contribution margin calculator](/tools/contribution-margin) shows how price and cost changes move that number.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter cost per unit (purchase or production cost including inward freight and non-creditable GST).
2. Enter selling price (excluding GST, since GST is collected for the government, not earned).
3. Add per-unit variable costs: packaging, payment fee (a percentage for cards/gateways; UPI P2M above ₹2,000 now carries MDR for larger merchants — check the [UPI MDR calculator](/tools/upi-mdr-calculator)), delivery subsidy, commission.
4. Enter monthly fixed costs — rent, salaries, electricity, software, loan EMIs.
5. Read gross margin, net margin, markup, contribution per unit and break-even units (and revenue). Adjust price to see the effect.

## GST and the margin

Keep GST out of margin math. If you sell at ₹1,180 inclusive at 18%, your revenue is ₹1,000; the ₹180 is passed on (net of input credit on purchases). Composition dealers, who cannot collect GST, must treat their 1% or 5% tax as a cost — the [GST calculator](/tools/gst-calculator) helps separate the two. The [GST-on-MRP guide](/blog/gst-on-mrp-inclusive-price-calculation) covers inclusive pricing.

## Improving margin without raising prices

- **Buy better**: negotiate a 3% cash discount or larger packs; the [purchase order](/tools/purchase-order) tool formalises terms.
- **Cut variable cost**: right-size packaging and use the [volumetric weight calculator](/tools/volumetric-weight) to avoid dimensional-weight surcharges on shipping.
- **Mix**: push higher-margin add-ons; a ₹30 dip at 70% margin lifts a ₹200 order's blended margin measurably.
- **Discount smartly**: use the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) to see the margin after stacked offers before printing tags.
- **Fixed-cost leverage**: once past break-even, each extra unit contributes the full contribution margin — volume matters more than a few rupees of price.

## FAQ

### What is a good profit margin for a small business in India?

It depends on the trade: kirana and FMCG retail run 8–15% gross, apparel 40–60%, restaurants 60–70% gross but 8–15% net, services 30–60% net. Compare with your break-even rather than a generic benchmark.

### Should selling price include GST in the calculation?

No. Use the price excluding GST as revenue; GST collected is a liability, not income. The calculator has a toggle to strip GST from an inclusive price.

### How do I include payment gateway fees?

Enter the fee percentage (typically 1.5–2% plus 18% GST on the fee for cards and gateways; zero for most UPI P2PM merchants). The calculator applies it per unit.

### What is contribution margin?

Selling price minus variable cost per unit — the amount each sale contributes toward fixed costs and, after break-even, profit.

### How do I calculate break-even revenue?

Fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio (contribution ÷ price). The calculator shows both units and revenue.
