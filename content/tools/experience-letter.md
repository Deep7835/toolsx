An experience letter (and its cousin, the relieving letter) is the document every employee needs for their next job's background check, and the one small employers most often delay or get wrong — missing dates, no designation, no letterhead, unsigned. This generator drafts a clean, professional **experience certificate or relieving letter** on your letterhead with the employee's name, designation(s), tenure, a short description of responsibilities and conduct, and the signatory's details, exported as a PDF ready to sign. It runs in the browser; nothing about your employees is uploaded.

## Experience letter vs relieving letter

- **Experience letter / certificate**: confirms that the person worked with you, in what role, for what period, and (optionally) how they performed. Issued on request, sometimes years later.
- **Relieving letter**: confirms that the employee has been relieved of duties on a specific date after completing notice and handover, with no dues pending. Issued at exit, and required by most new employers before joining.
- **Salary certificate**: separate, states the last drawn salary; combine only if the employee asks.

Under the Code on Wages, full and final settlement is due within two working days of the last day; the letters usually go out with it. The employee's side of that process is covered in [Notice period, last working day and full & final settlement](/blog/notice-period-and-full-and-final-settlement).

| Designation(s) and department | With dates if promoted |
| Tenure | "from 3 January 2022 to 30 September 2026" |
| Nature of work | 1–3 lines; factual, not evaluative unless positive |
| Conduct | "was found sincere and hardworking" is standard; avoid negative remarks — issue a plain letter instead |
| Closing | "We wish them success in future endeavours" |
| Signatory | Name, designation, signature, company seal |

Keep the tone neutral and factual. A letter is not a reference; if you cannot say something positive, state only role and dates.

## Legal and practical notes

- There is no central statute that compels an experience letter for every private employee, but Shops and Establishments rules in several states and standing orders in factories require a service certificate on request — and withholding it to force recovery of dues is a bad practice that courts frown on.
- Do not put confidential remuneration or disciplinary details in an experience letter.
- For interns, use the [internship certificate maker](/tools/internship-certificate) instead; the wording differs.
- Digital signatures are valid; a DSC or Aadhaar eSign is stronger than a pasted image for documents that may be verified later. See [Digital signatures in India](/blog/digital-signature-dsc-vs-aadhaar-esign).
- Background-verification agencies call the number on the letterhead, so make sure it is current.

## FAQ

### Is an employer legally required to give an experience letter?

Most state Shops and Establishments Acts and industrial standing orders require a service certificate on request. Even where not strictly mandated, refusing one is poor practice and often challenged.

### Can I mention that the employee left without notice?

Keep the experience letter factual — role and dates. Do not add negative remarks; if the exit was irregular, you may decline to issue a relieving letter until dues are settled, but state the reason in writing.

### Should the letter show the salary?

No. Issue a separate salary certificate if the employee asks for one.

### Can the letter be signed digitally?

Yes. A DSC-signed PDF is legally equivalent to a wet signature under the IT Act; a scanned signature image is commonly accepted but easier to dispute.

### How long should I keep copies?

Personnel records are generally kept for at least three years after exit, longer if there is a dispute or a statutory requirement in your industry.
