Percentages run every business conversation — "sales are up 18%", "give me 12% off", "what's 5% of ₹2,34,000", "margin dropped from 32% to 27%, that's 5 points, not 5%". This calculator handles the six percentage questions that come up daily: X% of Y, what percent X is of Y, percentage change between two numbers, increase or decrease a number by a percentage, reverse a percentage (find the original), and percentage-point differences — with instant results and the formula shown so you can explain it to whoever asked.

## The six calculations

| Question | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What is X% of Y? | Y × X ÷ 100 | 18% of ₹12,500 = ₹2,250 |
| X is what % of Y? | X ÷ Y × 100 | ₹3,400 of ₹20,000 = 17% |
| % change from A to B | (B − A) ÷ A × 100 | ₹80,000 → ₹94,000 = +17.5% |
| Increase/decrease Y by X% | Y × (1 ± X ÷ 100) | ₹1,499 less 30% = ₹1,049.30 |
| Original before a % change | B ÷ (1 ± X ÷ 100) | ₹1,180 after +18% → ₹1,000 |
| Percentage-point difference | B% − A% | 32% → 27% = −5 points (−15.6% relative) |

## How to use it

1. Pick the tab for the question you have.
2. Type the numbers; results update as you type, with the formula shown beneath.
3. Copy the result or the formula line into a message, a quote or a [social post](/tools/social-post).
4. Chain calculations — the result of one tab can be pushed into the next (for example, add 18% GST after a 30% discount).

## Where each one shows up

- **Discounts and GST**: reverse-percentage is the one people get wrong. A price of ₹1,180 including 18% GST has a base of ₹1,000, not ₹967.60. The [GST calculator](/tools/gst-calculator) and [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) apply this automatically.
- **Growth reporting**: month-on-month and year-on-year change; the [revenue growth calculator](/tools/revenue-growth) annualises it and the [CAGR calculator](/tools/cagr-calculator) smooths multi-year growth.
- **Margins**: margin is profit ÷ price; markup is profit ÷ cost — the [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin) shows both.
- **Statutory rates**: 12% PF on basic, 0.75% ESI, 8.33% bonus, 1% or 2% TDS — small percentages of large payrolls. The [team payroll calculator](/tools/team-payroll) applies them.
- **Interest**: 18% p.a. on late GST, 1% a month on advance-tax shortfalls, 3× bank rate under the MSMED Act — see [late payment interest](/tools/late-payment-interest).
- **Shares and splits**: profit sharing between partners, commission splits, area-based charges in a society.

## Percent vs percentage points

If your conversion rate moves from 4% to 5%, it rose by **one percentage point** and by **25%** relative. Reports that mix the two mislead. The calculator shows both whenever you compare two percentages, which matters for margins, interest rates and tax rates alike.

## Quick mental checks

- 10% = move the decimal one place; 5% = half of that; 1% = two places.
- 18% GST = 10% + 5% + 3% (₹1,000 → 100 + 50 + 30 = ₹180).
- A 20% discount followed by 20% markup does not return to the start: ₹100 → ₹80 → ₹96.
- To undo a p% increase you need a p ÷ (1 + p) decrease: undoing +25% needs −20%.

## FAQ

### How do I calculate percentage change?

(New − Old) ÷ Old × 100. A rise from ₹50,000 to ₹62,000 is (12,000 ÷ 50,000) × 100 = 24%.

### How do I find the original price before GST?

Divide by (1 + rate). For 18%: ₹1,180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000. Do not subtract 18% of the inclusive price.

### What is the difference between percent and percentage point?

Percentage points are the arithmetic difference between two percentages (5% − 4% = 1 point); percent change is the relative change (1 ÷ 4 = 25%).

### How do I compute a percentage of a percentage?

Multiply the decimals: 30% of 20% = 0.3 × 0.2 = 6%.

### Can I use it for marks or exam percentages?

Yes — "X is what % of Y" gives marks obtained ÷ total × 100; the tool is general-purpose.
