A 500-gram cushion in a 40 × 30 × 20 cm box ships as **4.8 kg**. Couriers charge by the space a parcel takes as much as by what it weighs, and volumetric (dimensional) weight is how they measure it: length × width × height in centimetres divided by a carrier-specific divisor. This calculator computes volumetric weight for Delhivery, Blue Dart, DTDC, Xpressbees, Ecom Express, Shadowfax, DHL/FedEx and air cargo, compares it with actual weight to find the **chargeable weight**, and shows what a smaller box would save.

## The formula and the divisors

Volumetric weight (kg) = L × W × H (cm) ÷ divisor.

| Carrier / mode | Divisor |
|---|---|
| Delhivery, Ekart, DTDC, Xpressbees, most aggregators (surface) | 5000 |
| Blue Dart, DHL, FedEx domestic | 4000 |
| Ecom Express, Shadowfax | 4500 |
| International air cargo | 6000 |
| Amazon/Flipkart fulfilment fees (typical) | 5000 |

Chargeable weight = the higher of actual and volumetric, rounded **up** to the carrier's slab (usually 0.5 kg). The 40 × 30 × 20 cm box at divisor 5000 is 4.8 kg → billed as 5 kg; at Blue Dart's 4000 it is 6 kg.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter the parcel's length, width and height in cm and its actual weight.
2. Pick the carrier divisor (or enter a custom one from your rate card).
3. Read the volumetric weight, the chargeable weight after rounding, and — if you enter a rate per kg or per 500 g — the shipping cost.
4. Try smaller dimensions; the tool shows the saving per parcel and per 100 parcels.
5. Use the multi-parcel mode for a B2B consignment: total chargeable weight for several boxes.

## Why box size is the cheapest optimisation

Cutting the cushion's box to 32 × 26 × 12 cm gives 2 kg volumetric — a 3 kg reduction, or roughly ₹60–120 per parcel on domestic surface rates. Over 500 parcels a month that is ₹30,000–60,000. Steps that pay for themselves in a week:

- Stock three or four box sizes and pick the tightest fit; avoid one "universal" carton.
- Use vacuum bags for soft goods, corrugated mailers for flat items, and poly bags where damage risk is low.
- Measure the packed parcel, not the product; filler and folded flaps add centimetres.
- Round dimensions honestly — carriers re-measure at the hub and bill the difference plus a penalty.

## Air, international and B2B

Air cargo uses a 6000 divisor (1 cbm = 167 kg) and the same higher-of rule; ocean LCL freight is charged per cubic metre instead. For domestic B2B on surface transport (part-truck load), rates are per kg with a volumetric equivalent of about 1 cbm = 250–350 kg depending on the transporter — ask for the "CFT rate". Put the chargeable weight on the [shipping label](/tools/shipping-label) and the [delivery challan](/tools/delivery-challan) so there is no argument at the hub.

## FAQ

### What is volumetric weight?

The weight equivalent of the space a parcel occupies: L × W × H in cm ÷ a divisor (usually 5000 in India). Carriers bill the higher of actual and volumetric weight.

### Which divisor should I use?

5000 for most Indian surface couriers and marketplace fulfilment; 4000 for Blue Dart, DHL and FedEx domestic; 6000 for international air. Check your rate card — the calculator lets you enter any divisor.

### Is chargeable weight rounded?

Yes, up to the carrier's slab, typically 0.5 kg. A 1.1 kg chargeable weight is billed as 1.5 kg.

### Do couriers actually measure boxes?

Yes, hubs use dimensioning scanners and bill the difference with a penalty if your declared dimensions are lower. Measure the packed parcel accurately.

### Does the divisor apply to length in inches?

The formula here uses centimetres. For inches, the equivalent divisor is 305 (L × W × H in inches ÷ 305 ≈ kg at divisor 5000); the calculator has a unit toggle.
