An ID card tells customers who is staff, tells security who may enter, and tells a hospital who to call if something goes wrong. This creator designs **printable employee ID cards** at standard CR80 size (85.6 × 54 mm) with a photo, name, designation, employee ID, department, blood group, emergency contact, validity and a QR or barcode carrying the ID — front and back — and exports them as PNG or a PDF sheet for PVC printing or lamination. It runs in your browser; photos and details are never uploaded.

## What goes on the card

| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Company logo and name | Address, phone, "if found, please return to" |
| Employee photo (35 × 45 mm ratio) | Emergency contact name and number |
| Full name in large type | Blood group |
| Designation and department | Validity dates and card number |
| Employee ID | QR/barcode for attendance or access |
| Signature strip (optional) | Terms: "Property of the company; must be surrendered on exit" |

Colour-coding the header by department or role (staff, contractor, visitor, intern) lets security read access rights at a glance.

## How to use the creator

1. Set up the company profile — logo, name, address, helpline — once; it is remembered.
2. Enter the employee's details and upload a photo; the tool crops it to the standard ratio and removes uneven margins.
3. Choose the template and colour; toggle blood group, emergency contact, validity and the QR/barcode.
4. Preview front and back, then download PNG (300 dpi) or a PDF sheet laid out ten cards to an A4 for lamination.
5. For a team, batch mode takes a list (name, designation, ID) and one folder of photos.

## Printing options

- **PVC cards** from a local ID-card printer (₹30–80 a card, often with lanyard) look most professional; send the 300 dpi PNG front and back.
- **Laminated paper** on 300 gsm card with a 125-micron pouch works for small teams and interns.
- **Lanyard vs clip**: clips for staff who lean over counters or machines; lanyards for offices and events.
- Use the [barcode generator](/tools/barcode-generator) if your attendance system needs Code 128 rather than QR.

## Data protection and practicalities

- Print only what is needed on the outside; blood group and emergency contact belong on the back.
- Do not print Aadhaar, PAN or home address on a card worn in public.
- Under the DPDP Act, employee photos and contact details are personal data: collect them with notice, keep them for the purpose of the card, and delete on exit — the practical checklist is in [DPDP Rules 2025 for small businesses](/blog/dpdp-rules-2025-checklist-for-small-websites).
- Set a validity period (one to three years) and retrieve cards at exit; record it in the [experience letter](/tools/experience-letter) file.
- Visitor and contractor cards should look different from staff cards.

## FAQ

### What is the standard ID card size?

CR80 — 85.6 × 54 mm, the same as a debit card. PVC printers and lamination pouches are built for it.

### Do I need to include the blood group?

It is optional but common in factories, hospitals and field roles. Put it on the back with the emergency contact.

### Can the QR code work with our attendance system?

The QR encodes the employee ID as plain text; most attendance apps and scanners can read it. If your system needs a barcode, switch to Code 128.

### Is a photo mandatory?

For a security or access card, yes. The tool crops uploaded photos to the passport ratio automatically.

### Are the photos stored anywhere?

No. Images stay in your browser session and are rendered into the card locally.
