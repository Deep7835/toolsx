Gross margin flatters; net profit margin tells the truth. After cost of goods, rent, salaries, marketing, payment fees, interest, depreciation and tax, what percentage of every ₹100 of sales actually stays with the business? For many Indian small businesses the honest answer is 3–8%, which is why a 5% discount or a 2% payment fee is not a small thing. This calculator computes net profit and net margin from revenue and each expense head, shows operating margin on the way, and lets you test what a price change or a cost cut does to the bottom line.

## The formula

Net profit = Revenue − COGS − Operating expenses − Interest − Depreciation − Tax.
Net margin = Net profit ÷ Revenue × 100.

Revenue ₹1.2 crore, COGS ₹72 lakh, operating expenses ₹30 lakh, interest ₹4 lakh, depreciation ₹6 lakh, tax ₹2.4 lakh → net profit ₹5.6 lakh, net margin **4.7%**. The same business shows a 40% gross margin and a 15% operating (EBITDA) margin — the [EBITDA calculator](/tools/ebitda-calculator) and [gross margin calculator](/tools/gross-margin) give those layers.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter revenue excluding GST.
2. Enter COGS, then operating expenses by head — rent, salaries (loaded, from the [employee cost calculator](/tools/employee-cost)), utilities, marketing, payment and marketplace fees, software, insurance, repairs.
3. Enter interest, depreciation (from the [depreciation calculator](/tools/depreciation-calculator)) and income tax.
4. Read gross, operating and net margin, and the rupee profit.
5. Use the what-if sliders: +3% price, −2% COGS, −₹20,000 rent — and see the net margin move.

## Why net margin is thin, and where the leaks are

- **Owner's salary** left out of expenses makes margin look better than it is; include a market salary.
- **Payment costs**: gateway fees at 2% + GST, and UPI MDR for P2M merchants above ₹2,000 from October 2026 ([UPI MDR calculator](/tools/upi-mdr-calculator)).
- **Returns and wastage** on marketplaces and in food; count them in COGS.
- **Interest** on working-capital loans and late GST — the [late payment interest](/tools/late-payment-interest) tool shows how fast 18% adds up.
- **Discounts** given as a habit rather than a decision; check every offer with the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator).
- **Tax**: profit under 44AD is presumed at 6–8% of turnover regardless of your real margin; if your actual net margin is lower, regular books may cut tax — see [Which ITR form to file](/blog/which-itr-form-small-business-freelancer).

## Improving the bottom line

A 4.7% net margin doubles if you can do any two of: raise prices 3%, cut COGS 3%, or cut operating expenses 10%. Small businesses usually find the third easiest — renegotiating rent, automating billing (the [GST invoice generator](/tools/gst-invoice) is free), cutting unused subscriptions — and the first hardest, though a 3% price rise on a differentiated product rarely loses customers. ## FAQ

### What is the difference between gross, operating and net margin?

Gross margin is after cost of goods; operating margin is after all operating expenses but before interest and tax; net margin is after everything, including tax.

### Should I include my own salary?

Yes, at a market rate. Otherwise the margin overstates the business's real profitability and misleads any buyer or lender.

### Is GST part of revenue?

No. Use revenue excluding GST, and exclude GST paid on purchases that you claim as input credit.

### What net margin should a small shop target?

Grocery 3–5%, apparel 5–10%, food service 8–12%, services 15%+. Consistency and cash conversion matter as much as the percentage.

### How do I improve net margin fastest?

Cut the largest controllable expense (often rent or discounts), raise prices modestly on differentiated items, and reduce payment and interest costs. The what-if sliders show which lever moves the margin most.
