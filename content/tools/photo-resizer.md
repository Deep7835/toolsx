A 6 MB photo from a phone camera is fine for a frame and wrong for everything a business does with it: WhatsApp compresses it into mush, marketplaces reject it, the website loads in five seconds on a customer's 4G, and the email bounces. This resizer and compressor brings product and catalogue photos down to the **right dimensions and file size for each channel** — WhatsApp, Instagram, Amazon/Flipkart/Meesho, your website, GST invoices and email — in your browser, with no upload, no watermark and batch processing for a whole catalogue.

## Sizes that each channel wants

| Channel | Dimensions | Target size |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp catalogue / status | 1080 × 1080 (square) or 1080 × 1920 (status) | Under 1 MB |
| Instagram feed / Reels cover | 1080 × 1350 (4:5) / 1080 × 1920 | Under 1 MB |
| Amazon product image | 1600 × 1600 minimum on the longest side (2000+ for zoom), pure white background | Under 10 MB, JPEG |
| Flipkart / Meesho | 1000 × 1000 or larger, square | Under 2 MB |
| Website product photo | 1200 px wide, WebP | 80–200 KB |
| Website hero/banner | 1920 × 800 | Under 300 KB |
| Invoice/letterhead logo | 600 px wide, PNG with transparency | Under 100 KB |
| Email attachment | 1200 px wide | Under 500 KB |

## How to use the tool

1. Drop one image or a folder of them (JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC from iPhones).
2. Pick a preset — WhatsApp, Instagram, Amazon, website, email — or set width, height and crop (fit, fill, square) manually.
3. Choose the output format: JPEG for photos, PNG for logos with transparency, WebP for the web.
4. Move the quality slider; the tool shows the resulting file size live. 80–85% JPEG quality is visually lossless for products.
5. Download individually or as a ZIP; file names are preserved with a suffix.

## Practical tips

- Keep an original at full resolution; resize copies per channel.
- Marketplaces want the product filling 85% of the frame on white — crop before resizing.
- Strip EXIF (the tool does by default) so location data from your phone does not ship with product photos.
- Name files descriptively (`blue-cotton-kurta-front.jpg`); it helps image search and your own catalogue.
- For print (labels, standees), do not compress — export PNG at 300 dpi from the source instead; the [price tag generator](/tools/price-tag-generator) and [UPI standee](/tools/upi-standee) handle their own resolution.

## FAQ

### Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Resizing and compression run in your browser; images never leave your device.

### JPEG, PNG or WebP?

JPEG for photographs (smallest at good quality), PNG for logos and graphics needing transparency, WebP for websites (30% smaller than JPEG at the same quality; supported by all current browsers).

### What quality setting should I use?

80–85% for JPEG and WebP is indistinguishable from the original for product photos and cuts file size by 70–90%.

### Can I resize many photos at once?

Yes. Drop a folder or select multiple files; the same preset applies to all and the results download as a ZIP.

### Does it handle iPhone HEIC files?

Yes — HEIC is converted to JPEG or WebP in the browser.
