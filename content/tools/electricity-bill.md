Landlords who sub-meter tenants, PG and hostel operators, shared-office managers, and shopkeepers who pass electricity charges to a sub-let counter all need the same thing: a clear, itemised electricity bill that shows meter readings, units consumed, slab-wise energy charges, fixed charges, taxes and a due date. This generator lets you create that bill for your own premises in a familiar DISCOM-style layout, with your name as the biller, and download it as a PDF. It is a **billing template for your own tenants and records** — not a copy of any electricity company's bill, and it should never be used to imitate one.

## Where a private electricity bill is used

- **Sub-metered tenants** in a house, shop complex or PG — the landlord pays the DISCOM and recovers each unit's share.
- **Coworking and shared kitchens** where machines run on a member's sub-meter.
- **Housing societies** that bill common-area electricity to flats along with maintenance; pair it with the [society maintenance invoice](/tools/society-maintenance).
- **Factories and warehouses** splitting a single HT connection between units or contractors.
- **Rent agreements** that say "electricity as per actual consumption at ₹X per unit" and need a monthly statement to back the claim.

## How units, slabs and charges work

A DISCOM bill has three layers, and your tenant bill should mirror them so it is easy to reconcile:

| Component | How it is computed |
|---|---|
| Units (kWh) | Current reading − previous reading, times the meter's multiplying factor (usually 1) |
| Energy charge | Units × tariff; many states use rising slabs (e.g. 0–100, 101–300, 301+ units) |
| Fixed / demand charge | Per kW of sanctioned load, per month |
| Electricity duty / tax | A state percentage on the energy charge (varies roughly 5–16%) |
| Fuel adjustment (FPPCA/FAC) | Per-unit surcharge that changes monthly |
| Arrears, late fee, subsidy | Carried from earlier bills or state schemes |

When you sub-bill, the simplest fair method is the **blended rate**: your DISCOM bill total ÷ total units, applied to the tenant's units, plus their share of fixed charges. The generator supports both a flat per-unit rate and slab entry.

## How to use the generator

1. Enter the biller (you or your society) and the consumer (tenant) with the premises address and meter number.
2. Enter previous and current readings and the reading dates; the tool computes units and the billing period.
3. Set the tariff — a single rate or slabs — plus fixed charges and any duty percentage.
4. Add arrears or a late fee if applicable and set the due date.
5. Download the PDF and send it. Attach a photo of the meter reading for transparency.

## GST and tax treatment

Electricity supplied by a DISCOM is exempt from GST. When a landlord recovers electricity charges from a tenant **at actual cost as a pure agent** — same units, same rate, backed by the DISCOM bill — the recovery stays outside GST. If you charge a markup, bundle it into rent, or bill a flat "power charge", it becomes part of the taxable rent or service and attracts GST at 18% on a commercial lease. Keep the DISCOM bill and your sub-bill together to support the pure-agent position. Reimbursements should be documented with a [payment receipt](/tools/payment-receipt) once paid.

## Tips for fair sub-metering

- Use certified sub-meters and note the meter serial on every bill.
- Take readings on the same date each month, photograph the display, and share the photo with the bill.
- State the rate basis in the rent agreement ("blended DISCOM rate for the month").
- For solar rooftop with net metering, show import and export units separately.
- Pass on **subsidies** if the connection is subsidised; charging full tariff on a subsidised meter is a common dispute.

Landlords issuing these bills alongside rent should also read [HRA exemption: rent receipts, landlord PAN](/blog/hra-exemption-rent-receipts-landlord-pan), since tenants will ask for both documents together at tax time.

## FAQ

### Can I use this to make a bill that looks like my electricity board's?

No, and you should not. The template is for private sub-billing by a landlord, society or operator, in your own name. Imitating a DISCOM's bill format or logo can amount to forgery.

### Is a landlord allowed to charge more per unit than the DISCOM rate?

Rent-control and consumer laws in several states restrict landlords to actual cost. Charging a markup can also bring the recovery under GST. The safe approach is cost recovery with the DISCOM bill attached.

### How do I calculate a tenant's share without a sub-meter?

Use an agreed proportion — floor area or number of appliances — and state it in the agreement. A ₹1,500 sub-meter pays for itself in the first dispute avoided.

### Do I charge GST on electricity recovered from a commercial tenant?

Not if it is recovered at actual cost as a pure agent. If it is a fixed monthly charge or included in rent, it is taxable with the rent.

### Can the bill be used as address proof?

A private sub-bill is not an official utility document and is not accepted as address proof by banks or government offices.
