Growth is the one number every owner quotes and few compute the same way. Month-on-month, year-on-year, quarter-over-quarter, annualised, per store, per channel — each answers a different question, and mixing them makes a bad month look like a trend or a Diwali spike look like a breakthrough. This calculator computes **revenue growth rate** between any two periods, annualises it, handles seasonality by comparing the same period last year, and produces a clean growth table you can drop into a loan proposal or a partner update.

## The formulas

- **Period growth** = (Current − Previous) ÷ Previous × 100. ₹9.2 lakh this month vs ₹8 lakh last month → +15%.
- **Year-on-year (YoY)** compares with the same month last year, removing seasonality: ₹9.2 lakh vs ₹7.5 lakh last September → +22.7%.
- **Annualised** monthly growth = (1 + m)¹² − 1; 3% a month is 42.6% a year, not 36%.
- **Multi-year** growth is best expressed as CAGR — see the [CAGR calculator](/tools/cagr-calculator).

Always compare revenue **excluding GST**; a slab change (like the September 2025 rationalisation) alters inclusive figures without any change in your sales.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter the current and previous period revenue (or paste up to 24 periods).
2. Choose the comparison: previous period, same period last year, or a base period.
3. Read the growth rate, the absolute change, and the annualised rate.
4. In table mode, see MoM and YoY side by side for each period and export CSV.
5. Split by product line, channel or store to find what is actually growing.

## Reading growth honestly

- **Seasonality**: October is not comparable to September in retail; use YoY for festive months.
- **Price vs volume**: a 10% price rise with flat units is 10% growth with no new customers; track units too.
- **One-offs**: a single ₹5 lakh corporate order distorts a ₹20 lakh month; note it.
- **Channel shifts**: growth on Amazon that cannibalises counter sales is not growth; the [seller pricing workflow](/tools/seller-pricing) shows what each channel keeps.
- **Base effects**: recovering from a bad month produces a large percentage that says little.

## Growth and the rest of the business

- **Profit**: revenue up 20% with net margin down from 12% to 8% means profit fell. Track the [net profit margin](/tools/net-profit-margin) alongside.
- **Cash**: growth consumes working capital — more stock, more receivables. Watch [inventory turnover](/tools/inventory-turnover) and collection days, and the 45-day rule if you are the supplier ([Section 43B(h)](/blog/section-43b-h-45-day-payment-rule)).
- **Capacity**: 25% growth on 90% [capacity utilization](/tools/capacity-utilization) means capex or missed orders.
- **Compliance thresholds**: crossing ₹20 lakh triggers GST registration, ₹5 crore triggers e-invoicing and 6-digit HSN, ₹1 crore (business) or ₹50 lakh (profession) triggers tax audit and TDS obligations; see [E-invoicing in 2026](/blog/e-invoicing-2026-are-you-covered).
- **Loans**: banks look for 2–3 years of consistent growth; a clean YoY table strengthens the file.

## Presenting growth

For a partner deck or bank proposal: show revenue for the last 8–12 quarters, YoY growth per quarter, and a short note on drivers (new SKUs, new channel, price). Add the profit line and the [budget variance](/tools/budget-variance) against plan so the story is credible.

## FAQ

### How do I calculate revenue growth rate?

(Current period revenue − previous period revenue) ÷ previous period revenue × 100, using revenue excluding GST.

### What is a good growth rate for a small business?

Sustained 15–25% a year is healthy for an established small business in India; new businesses can grow faster off small bases. Profitable growth matters more than the rate.

### Should I use MoM or YoY?

YoY for seasonal businesses and for annual reporting; MoM for spotting short-term momentum, ideally as a 3-month moving average.

### How do I annualise a monthly growth rate?

(1 + monthly rate)^12 − 1. Simply multiplying by 12 understates compounding.

### Does GST count in revenue?

No. Use taxable value (revenue excluding GST) so rate changes and inclusive pricing do not distort growth.
