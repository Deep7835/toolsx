Ten thousand people saw the ad, 400 clicked, 90 messaged, 30 asked for a quote, 9 paid. Each step lost most of the people from the one before — and the step that lost the *most* is where the next rupee of effort should go. This funnel calculator maps your customer journey stage by stage, computes the **conversion rate at each step and end to end**, finds the biggest drop-off, and simulates what happens to orders and revenue if you improve one stage by a few points.

## How a funnel is read

| Stage | Count | Step conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions / footfall | 10,000 | — |
| Clicks / enquiries | 400 | 4% |
| Conversations / visits | 90 | 22.5% |
| Quotes / carts | 30 | 33% |
| Orders | 9 | 30% |

End-to-end conversion is 0.09%. The weakest step here is impressions → clicks (4%): better creative or targeting would move it, and a rise to 6% lifts orders to 13.5 with no other change. Compare that with raising quote → order from 30% to 40% (12 orders) — both worth doing, but the numbers say which first.

## How to use the calculator

1. Name your stages (defaults: Impressions, Clicks, Leads, Qualified, Quotes, Customers) and enter the count at each.
2. Read the step-wise conversion, the cumulative conversion and the largest drop-off, which is highlighted.
3. Enter average order value to see revenue at the bottom and the rupee value of each lost stage.
4. Move the **what-if** slider on any stage to see the effect downstream.
5. Save funnels per channel — Meta ads, Google Business calls, marketplace listings, walk-ins — and compare.

## Typical funnels for Indian small businesses

- **Instagram → WhatsApp → order**: impressions, profile visits, link taps to the [wa.me link](/tools/whatsapp-direct), conversations, orders. The chat stage usually leaks most — response time and a clear price fix it (see [first response time](/tools/first-response-time)).
- **Google Business Profile → call → visit → sale**: profile views, calls/directions, walk-ins, purchases. Reviews and photos move the first step; the [review request builder](/tools/google-review) feeds it.
- **Marketplace listing**: impressions, product page views, add-to-cart, orders, net of returns. Photos and price band move page views → cart; the [seller pricing workflow](/tools/seller-pricing) sets the price.
- **B2B**: leads, qualified, quote sent ([service quote builder](/tools/service-quote)), negotiation, won. Track [win rate](/tools/win-rate) and [sales cycle length](/tools/sales-cycle) alongside.

## Fixing the leakiest stage

- **Top (attention)**: creative, hook, targeting, offer; measure with [engagement rate](/tools/engagement-rate).
- **Middle (consideration)**: speed of reply, clear pricing, social proof, easy next step.
- **Bottom (decision)**: payment friction (put a [UPI QR](/tools/upi-standee) or link in the chat), delivery clarity, a deadline on the offer.
- **After the sale**: retention and referral turn one order into several — the [retention rate](/tools/retention-rate) and [referral rate](/tools/referral-rate) tools measure that second funnel.

## FAQ

### How many stages should a funnel have?

Four to six. Fewer hides where the loss happens; more becomes impossible to keep counts for. Match stages to actions you can actually measure.

### What is a good end-to-end conversion rate?

For paid social to purchase, 0.5–2% is typical for D2C; for Google Business calls to walk-in purchases, 20–40%. Compare stages against your own history rather than generic benchmarks.

### Which stage should I fix first?

The one with the largest absolute drop relative to what is achievable — the calculator highlights it, and the what-if slider shows the downstream effect of each candidate.

### How do I get the counts?

Ad platforms give impressions and clicks; WhatsApp Business gives conversations; your quote register and billing software give quotes and orders. Consistency matters more than precision.

### Can I use this for offline funnels?

Yes — footfall (a door counter or an estimate), engaged customers, trials/fittings, purchases. The maths is the same.
