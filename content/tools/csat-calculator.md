"Was everything okay?" gets a nod from everyone; a one-question survey after the order gets the truth. Customer satisfaction score (CSAT) is the share of customers who rated a specific interaction — a delivery, a repair, a meal, a support chat — as satisfied. It is quick to collect on WhatsApp, easy to explain to staff, and the fastest feedback loop a small business can run. This calculator computes CSAT from your survey counts on a 1–5 (or 1–3, 1–7, 1–10) scale, shows the distribution, the trend, and the margin of error for small samples.

## The formula

CSAT = (Number of satisfied responses — typically 4 and 5 on a 5-point scale) ÷ Total responses × 100.

Out of 85 responses last month: 39 gave 5, 27 gave 4, 12 gave 3, 5 gave 2, 2 gave 1 → CSAT = 66 ÷ 85 = **77.6%**. Mean score 4.13 out of 5. With 85 responses the margin of error is about ±9 points, so a move from 78% to 74% next month is noise; a move to 65% is a signal.

## How to use the calculator

1. Choose the scale and enter the count of responses at each level (or paste the ratings).
2. Read CSAT, the mean score, the distribution and the sample's margin of error.
3. Add previous periods to see the trend.
4. Segment by touchpoint — delivery, in-store, support, product — or by staff member or branch.
5. Pair with an open text question; the numbers say *how much*, the comments say *why*.

## Collecting CSAT in India without software

- **WhatsApp** after the order: "How was your experience? Reply 1–5." Automate a template on WhatsApp Business (consented, transactional).
- **QR at the counter or on the bill**, made with the [URL QR generator](/tools/url-qr), pointing to a one-question Google Form.
- **Thermal receipt footer** from the [payment receipt](/tools/payment-receipt) tool with the QR.
- **Ask within 24 hours** of the interaction; response rates drop fast.
- Keep it to one rating and one optional comment; long surveys get ignored.

## CSAT vs NPS vs reviews

- **CSAT** measures a transaction: was *this* delivery good?
- **[NPS](/tools/nps-calculator)** measures the relationship: would you recommend us? Ask it quarterly, not after every order.
- **Google reviews** are public CSAT with marketing value; ask satisfied customers (4–5) for one via the [review request builder](/tools/google-review), but never filter who sees the review link.
- **[First contact resolution](/tools/fcr-rate)** and **[first response time](/tools/first-response-time)** are the operational drivers behind support CSAT.

## Acting on the score

- Below 70%: something structural — delivery times, product quality, staff behaviour. Read the 1–2 comments and fix the top issue this week.
- 70–85%: normal; work on the most common 3-rating reason.
- Above 85%: protect it; use it in marketing with permission, and watch for survey bias (only happy customers answering).
- Track CSAT next to [order accuracy](/tools/order-accuracy) and [on-time delivery](/tools/on-time-delivery); operational metrics usually move first.

## FAQ

### What is a good CSAT score?

75–85% is typical for retail and services; above 85% is excellent. Trends and comments matter more than the absolute figure.

### Which ratings count as "satisfied"?

The top two on a 5-point scale (4 and 5). On a 3-point scale, only the top; on a 10-point scale, 9–10 for a strict view or 7–10 for a lenient one — pick one and be consistent.

### How many responses do I need?

At least 30–50 per period for a usable number; the calculator shows the margin of error so you know when a change is real.

### CSAT or NPS — which should a small shop use?

Start with CSAT after transactions; it is easier to collect and act on. Add NPS quarterly once you have a customer list.

### How do I raise response rates?

Ask immediately after the interaction, on the channel the customer already used (WhatsApp), with one question and a thank-you. Rates of 20–40% are achievable.
