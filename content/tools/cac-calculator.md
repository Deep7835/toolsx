Every rupee spent on Instagram boosts, Google Ads, pamphlets, referral gifts and the salesperson's salary buys customers — the question is at what price each. Customer acquisition cost is that price: total sales and marketing spend in a period divided by the number of **new** customers won in it. Know it, and you know whether a ₹300 discount for first orders is generous or reckless, whether a campaign paid off, and how many orders a customer must place before you break even. This calculator computes CAC, blended and by channel, and compares it with customer value.

## The formula

CAC = (Marketing spend + Sales spend) ÷ New customers acquired.

A boutique spent ₹45,000 on Meta ads, ₹12,000 on a designer, ₹8,000 on printed flyers and ₹30,000 of a salesperson's time in September and won 190 new customers → CAC = 95,000 ÷ 190 = **₹500**. If a first order carries ₹420 of gross margin, the customer is not profitable until the second purchase — which is why [retention](/tools/retention-rate) is the other half of the calculation.

## What to include in spend

| Include | Often forgotten |
|---|---|
| Ad spend (Meta, Google, marketplace ads) | GST on ad invoices if not claimed as ITC |
| Agency and freelancer fees, creative, photos | Discounts and free shipping for first orders |
| Salaries of sales and marketing staff (loaded — see [employee cost](/tools/employee-cost)) | Referral rewards paid out |
| Tools: CRM, WhatsApp API, design software | Trade-show stalls, samples, listing fees |
| Printed material, standees, signage | Time the owner spends selling |

Count **new** customers only — repeat buyers are the return on earlier CAC, not a new cost.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter each spend item for the period (or just the totals) and the number of new customers.
2. Optionally split by channel — Meta, Google, WhatsApp referrals, walk-in — to see CAC per channel.
3. Enter average first-order margin and expected repeat purchases to see **payback** (orders to recover CAC) and the LTV:CAC ratio.
4. Track monthly; a rising CAC with flat conversion usually means audience fatigue or price competition.

## Benchmarks and the LTV:CAC rule

Small D2C brands in India see CAC of ₹300–1,500 on Meta depending on category; local services ₹100–800 per lead-turned-customer; B2B suppliers ₹2,000–20,000. The number itself matters less than the ratio: lifetime gross margin from a customer should be **at least 3× CAC**, and CAC should be recovered within a few purchases. If it is not, either raise retention, raise margin, or cut the channel.

## Cutting CAC without cutting growth

- **Referrals** are the cheapest channel — measure them with the [referral rate calculator](/tools/referral-rate) and reward them modestly.
- **Reviews** lift conversion for free; the [Google review request builder](/tools/google-review) makes asking routine.
- **Better conversion, same spend**: fix the funnel with the [lead conversion rate](/tools/lead-conversion) and [marketing funnel](/tools/marketing-funnel) tools before buying more traffic.
- **WhatsApp over paid ads** for repeat and reactivation — inbound chats are free; see [WhatsApp Business per-message pricing](/blog/whatsapp-business-per-message-pricing-2026).
- **Local SEO**: a complete Google Business Profile and [schema markup](/tools/schema-markup) bring customers at near-zero CAC — the 2026 checklist is in [Google AI Mode and local search](/blog/google-ai-mode-local-seo-for-indian-businesses-2026).

## FAQ

### What is a good CAC?

One that is comfortably below the gross margin a customer brings over their lifetime — a 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio is the usual target. Absolute numbers vary widely by category.

### Should salaries be included in CAC?

Yes, the sales and marketing share of them, at loaded cost. Leaving them out understates CAC, especially for B2B businesses that sell through people.

### Is CAC the same as cost per lead?

No. Cost per lead divides spend by enquiries; CAC divides by customers who actually bought. A ₹100 lead at 10% conversion is a ₹1,000 CAC.

### How do I attribute customers to channels?

Ask at checkout, use source tags in WhatsApp links, UTM parameters on URLs, and separate coupon codes per channel. Perfect attribution is impossible; consistent attribution is enough.

### How often should I calculate it?

Monthly, and per campaign for anything with a meaningful budget. Watch the trend rather than a single month.
