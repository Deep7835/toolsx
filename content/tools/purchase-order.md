A purchase order is the buyer's side of the paperwork: a numbered, dated document that tells a supplier exactly what you want, at what price, delivered where and by when, on what payment terms. Once the supplier accepts it, the PO is a contract. This generator builds a professional B2B purchase order with your GSTIN, the vendor's details, line items with HSN and GST, delivery and payment terms, and exports a PDF you can email or send on WhatsApp — no login, nothing uploaded.

## Why small businesses should use POs

Verbal orders work until the supplier ships 500 units instead of 50 or invoices at a rate you never agreed. A PO fixes quantity, rate, tax treatment, delivery date and place — so the supplier's [GST invoice](/tools/gst-invoice) can be checked against it line by line (the "three-way match" of PO, invoice and goods receipt that every auditor loves). It also gives your accounts team a document to book a commitment before the invoice arrives, and it is the reference number a vendor prints on the [delivery challan](/tools/delivery-challan) and invoice.

For MSME buyers there is another reason: if the supplier is a registered micro or small enterprise, **Section 43B(h)** allows your expense deduction only if you pay within the agreed credit period, capped at 45 days (15 days with no written agreement). A PO with clear payment terms is your evidence of the agreed period — the details are in [Section 43B(h): the 45-day payment rule](/blog/section-43b-h-45-day-payment-rule).

## What a purchase order should contain

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| Header | Your legal name, address, GSTIN, PO number, PO date, validity |
| Vendor | Name, address, GSTIN, contact person, vendor code if any |
| Ship-to / bill-to | Can differ — a warehouse vs the registered office |
| Line items | Description, HSN/SAC, quantity, unit, rate, discount, GST rate, amount |
| Totals | Taxable value, CGST/SGST or IGST, freight, round-off, grand total |
| Delivery terms | Date or lead time, Incoterm-style basis (ex-works, FOR destination), packing |
| Payment terms | Advance %, credit days, mode, bank details for supplier reference |
| Conditions | Quality, inspection, penalties for delay, cancellation, jurisdiction |
| Authorisation | Name and signature of the approver |

## Tips for cleaner procurement

- **Number POs by year and sequence** (PO-2627-0042) so the vendor and your team can quote them unambiguously.
- **Quote rates exclusive of GST** and state the rate per line; it prevents disputes when the slab changes, as it did in September 2025 — see [GST 2.0 rate changes](/blog/gst-2-0-new-rates-what-changed-for-small-business).
- **Specify tolerance** ("±5% quantity accepted") for bulk commodities.
- **Use a blanket PO** for repeat supplies over a period with call-offs against it.
- **Verify the vendor's GSTIN** before the first order so your ITC is safe: [How to verify a GSTIN](/blog/how-to-verify-gstin-number-format).

## FAQ

### Is a purchase order legally binding?

A PO is an offer; the supplier's acceptance — written confirmation, or shipping the goods — creates a contract on the PO's terms. Cancellation after acceptance can attract claims, so include a cancellation clause.

### Does a PO need GST details?

It is not a GST document, but showing the GST rate and both GSTINs lets the supplier invoice correctly and lets you check the invoice against it. Always include them for B2B purchases.

### Should the PO amount include GST?

Show taxable value, tax and total separately. Business buyers reclaim GST as ITC, so the taxable value is the number that matters for budgets.

### What if the invoice differs from the PO?

Ask the supplier for a corrected invoice or a credit/debit note before paying. Paying an invoice that does not match your PO weakens any later claim.

### Can I use this for services?

Yes — use SAC codes instead of HSN and describe the scope, milestones and acceptance criteria in the conditions box. For projects with labour and material, pair it with the supplier's [service quote](/tools/service-quote).
