Every website, app and WhatsApp business that collects a name, a phone number or an order address now has a legal duty to say what it does with them. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules notified in 2025 require a clear notice, a stated purpose, a way to withdraw consent and a grievance contact — and Google, Meta and payment gateways will not approve accounts without a privacy policy URL. This generator drafts a **plain-language privacy policy** tailored to what your business actually collects, aligned to the DPDP framework, with the standard sections international services expect, exported as HTML or text for your site.

## What the DPDP Act expects from a small business

| Requirement | What your policy must do |
|---|---|
| Notice | Tell people what personal data you collect and for what purpose, in clear language (and in the languages of the Eighth Schedule if you serve those users) |
| Consent | Explain how consent is given and withdrawn — cookie banner, form checkbox, WhatsApp opt-in |
| Purpose limitation | Use data only for the stated purpose; delete it when the purpose ends |
| Rights | Access, correction, erasure and grievance redressal; nomination |
| Grievance officer | A contact (name/role and email) and response timeline |
| Security | A statement of reasonable safeguards |
| Children | Verifiable parental consent for under-18s; no tracking or targeted ads to children |
| Breach notification | Tell affected users and the Data Protection Board |

The Rules are being implemented in phases; the practical checklist for small websites, apps and WhatsApp businesses is in [DPDP Rules 2025: the compliance checklist](/blog/dpdp-rules-2025-checklist-for-small-websites).

## How to use the generator

1. Enter your business name, website/app, contact email and the grievance officer.
2. Tick what you collect: contact details, order and delivery data, payment (via a gateway), account logins, location, device/analytics, cookies, marketing preferences, employee/applicant data.
3. Tick what you use: analytics (Google Analytics, Meta Pixel), payment gateways, courier partners, email/SMS/WhatsApp providers, cloud hosting; the policy names each category of "data processor".
4. Set retention periods (order data for tax records: 8 years; marketing consent until withdrawn) and whether you serve children.
5. Generate, read it once, edit the parts that are specific to you, and publish at `/privacy-policy`. Link it from the footer, forms, the cookie banner and your app-store listing.

## FAQ

### Is a privacy policy mandatory in India?

Yes for anyone processing digital personal data under the DPDP Act, and it was already required under the IT (Reasonable Security Practices) Rules 2011. Payment gateways, app stores and ad platforms also require one.

### Is the generated policy legally sufficient?

It covers the DPDP requirements and standard international sections for a typical small business. Businesses handling sensitive data at scale, health data, or children's data should have a lawyer review it.

### Do I need a grievance officer?

Yes — the DPDP Act requires a contact for grievances and the IT Rules require a grievance officer for intermediaries. For a small business this can be the owner.

### What about cookies?

State what cookies you use (essential, analytics, marketing) and how users can decline. Analytics and marketing cookies should load only after consent.

### How often should I update it?

Whenever you add a new data use, tool or processor, and at least annually. Keep a "last updated" date at the top.
