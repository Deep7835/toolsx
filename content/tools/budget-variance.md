A budget is a plan; a variance report is the conversation with reality. Sales 8% under plan, rent on target, marketing 40% over, salaries 5% over because of the bonus month — laid out line by line, variances tell you where the year is slipping and whether it is a price problem, a volume problem or a spending problem. This calculator compares **budgeted and actual figures for revenue and each expense head**, shows the variance in rupees and percent, flags favourable and unfavourable lines, and computes the net effect on profit — for a month, a quarter or the year to date.

## How variances are read

| Line | Favourable when | Unfavourable when |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Actual > Budget | Actual < Budget |
| Expenses | Actual < Budget | Actual > Budget |
| Profit | Actual > Budget | Actual < Budget |

Variance % = (Actual − Budget) ÷ Budget × 100. Sales budgeted ₹10 lakh, actual ₹9.2 lakh → −₹80,000, −8%, unfavourable. Marketing budgeted ₹50,000, actual ₹70,000 → +₹20,000, +40%, unfavourable. The calculator colours each line and totals the profit effect so a favourable expense variance that came from cutting marketing is seen next to the sales shortfall it may have caused.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter budget and actual for revenue, then for each expense head — COGS, rent, salaries, marketing, utilities, logistics, fees, other.
2. Read the variance per line in ₹ and %, marked favourable or unfavourable.
3. See the budgeted profit, actual profit and total variance.
4. Add a **year-to-date** column to separate a one-month blip from a trend.
5. Export CSV and paste into your monthly review; repeat monthly and compare variances over time.

## Digging into a variance

- **Revenue**: split into **price** and **volume** effects — (actual price − budget price) × actual units, and (actual units − budget units) × budget price. A 8% shortfall might be flat units and a 8% discount habit; the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) helps size it.
- **COGS**: rate variance (paid more per unit) vs usage variance (used more units — wastage, returns). The [gross margin calculator](/tools/gross-margin) tracks the ratio.
- **Salaries**: headcount vs rate vs overtime; a bonus month is timing, not overspend. The [team payroll calculator](/tools/team-payroll) gives the monthly figure.
- **Marketing**: overspend is fine if [ROAS](/tools/roas-calculator) and [CAC](/tools/cac-calculator) held; otherwise it is the first cut.
- **Timing variances** (an annual insurance premium paid in one month) wash out over the year — flag them rather than reacting.

## Building a budget worth comparing against

- Start from last year's actuals and the [revenue growth rate](/tools/revenue-growth), then adjust for known changes: rent escalation, the EPF ceiling rise, GST slab moves, a new hire.
- Budget by month, not by dividing the year by 12; festive months carry different sales and stock.
- Include statutory dates — GST, TDS, advance tax, bonus — so cash timing matches; the [GST filing calendar](/tools/gst-calendar) and [advance tax calculator](/tools/advance-tax) supply them.
- Check your forecasts afterwards with the [forecast accuracy](/tools/forecast-accuracy) tool; budgets improve when forecast error is measured.

## FAQ

### What is a budget variance?

The difference between what you planned (budget) and what happened (actual), in rupees and as a percentage of the budget, for each revenue and expense line.

### What counts as favourable?

Revenue above budget or expenses below budget. The calculator labels each line automatically.

### How often should I review variances?

Monthly for a small business, within a week of month end, with a year-to-date view alongside to separate timing from trend.

### What variance is significant?

A common threshold is ±5% or ±₹10,000, whichever is larger, for investigation. Set your own per line; rent needs no threshold, marketing may need a tighter one.

### Should I revise the budget mid-year?

Keep the original for accountability and add a reforecast column when circumstances change materially — a new store, a lost client, a regulation change.
