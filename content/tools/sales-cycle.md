The quote went out on the 3rd, the order came on the 29th; another took four months; a third was done in a week. Sales cycle length — the average time from first contact to payment or signed order — decides how much pipeline you need today to hit a target next quarter, when cash arrives, and whether a "slow month" is a real problem or just timing. This calculator computes the **average, median and range of your sales cycle** from deal dates, by segment, and turns it into a pipeline-timing plan.

## The formula

Sales cycle length = Sum of (Close date − First-contact date) for won deals ÷ Number of won deals.

Report the median too: one government order that took 180 days can drag a 25-day average to 40 and mislead planning. Track the stages if you can — enquiry → quote (response time), quote → decision (deliberation), decision → payment (collection) — because each has a different fix.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter first-contact and close dates for each won deal (paste a list from your quote register or CRM).
2. Read the average, median, shortest and longest cycles, and the distribution.
3. Segment by deal size, customer type (retail, SME, corporate, government) or source to see where cycles are long.
4. Enter your quarterly target and [win rate](/tools/win-rate); the tool shows when the pipeline must be created — a 45-day cycle means October's orders come from quotes sent by mid-August.
5. Use the [business days calculator](/tools/business-days) to translate calendar cycles into working days for staffing.

## Why cycles are long, and what shortens them

- **Slow first response**: the biggest avoidable delay; see [first response time](/tools/first-response-time).
- **Vague quotes** that trigger three rounds of clarification — fix with the [service quote builder](/tools/service-quote) and the checklist in [How to write a quotation](/blog/quotation-format-how-to-write-a-quotation).
- **Approval chains** at the customer: ask early who signs and what they need (GST invoice format, vendor registration, a [purchase order](/tools/purchase-order) template).
- **Advance payment friction**: a [proforma invoice](/tools/proforma-invoice) with a UPI QR or bank details shortens quote → payment by days.
- **No deadline**: quotes with a validity date and a reason to act (price rise, slot availability) close faster.
- **Collections**: if "close" means payment, the MSMED 45-day rule and Section 43B(h) give you leverage with large buyers — see [Section 43B(h): the 45-day payment rule](/blog/section-43b-h-45-day-payment-rule).

## Cycle length and cash flow

A 60-day cycle plus 30-day credit means a job quoted in July is paid in October — while wages, GST and rent are monthly. Multiply average deal value by deals in progress by cycle length to see working capital tied up, and plan overdrafts or advances accordingly. Interest on delayed payments can be quantified with the [late payment interest calculator](/tools/late-payment-interest).

## FAQ

### When does the sales cycle start and end?

Start at first meaningful contact (enquiry or first meeting); end at signed order or, if you prefer a cash view, at payment. Pick one definition and keep it.

### Should lost deals be included?

No. Cycle length uses won deals; time spent on lost deals is tracked separately as an efficiency measure.

### What is a good sales cycle length?

Retail and small services: days. SME B2B: 2–6 weeks. Corporate and government: 2–6 months. Shorter than your competitors is the useful benchmark.

### How do I use it for forecasting?

Quotes needed by a date = target ÷ average deal value ÷ win rate, created at least one cycle length before the target period.

### Median or average?

Median for planning typical deals; average for total capacity and cash planning. The calculator shows both.
