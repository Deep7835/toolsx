Gross margin is the first test of a price: after paying for the product itself — purchase cost or materials and direct labour — what percentage of the selling price is left to cover rent, salaries, marketing and profit? A product with a 20% gross margin cannot support a 25% marketplace commission; a service with a 70% gross margin can absorb a slow month. This calculator computes gross margin, gross profit and markup from cost and price, runs the reverse (the price needed for a target margin), and shows the effect of a discount or a cost increase.

## Definitions

- **Gross profit** = Revenue − Cost of goods sold (COGS).
- **Gross margin** = Gross profit ÷ Revenue × 100.
- **Markup** = Gross profit ÷ COGS × 100.

Buy at ₹640, sell at ₹1,000 (excluding GST): gross profit ₹360, gross margin **36%**, markup 56.25%. To hit a 45% margin, price = 640 ÷ (1 − 0.45) = ₹1,163.64. Margin and markup are often confused; the [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin) shows both alongside net margin and break-even.

## What goes into COGS

| Include | Exclude |
|---|---|
| Purchase price of goods, net of creditable GST | Rent, salaries of non-production staff, marketing |
| Inward freight, loading, customs duty | Depreciation on office assets |
| Raw materials, packaging that ships with the product | Interest, taxes |
| Direct labour in manufacturing or service delivery | Payment gateway fees (variable, but usually shown below gross) |
| Job-work charges, wastage | Warranty reserves (some businesses include them) |

For services, COGS is the direct cost of delivering — a designer's time on the project, a courier's fuel and driver — while owner salary and office costs sit below the line.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter cost per unit (or total COGS) and selling price (or total revenue), excluding GST.
2. Read gross profit, gross margin and markup.
3. Use **target margin** mode to find the price for a margin you want, or the maximum cost you can pay at a given price.
4. Apply a discount or a cost increase to see the new margin instantly — helpful before agreeing to a marketplace promotion.
5. Feed the margin into the [contribution margin](/tools/contribution-margin) and [net profit margin](/tools/net-profit-margin) calculators for the full picture.

## Benchmarks (India, indicative)

- Grocery and FMCG retail: 8–18%
- Electronics retail: 6–15%
- Apparel and footwear: 40–60%
- Restaurants and cafés (food cost 30–35%): 65–70%
- Handmade and D2C brands: 50–70%
- Pharmacies: 15–25%
- Professional services: 50–80%

A margin below the category norm usually points to buying badly or pricing on fear; above it, to a differentiated product or a small base of loyal customers.

## Protecting the margin

- **Buy in the right pack size** and negotiate cash discounts; the [purchase order](/tools/purchase-order) tool records terms.
- **Watch input GST**: a supplier who cannot pass ITC (composition dealer) is effectively 5–18% dearer.
- **Price after freight**: a ₹40 delivery on a ₹600 item is 6.7 points of margin — check [volumetric weight](/tools/volumetric-weight) surcharges before quoting free shipping.
- **Discount from margin, not from habit**: the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) shows the margin after an offer.
- **Reprice on cost changes** quickly; a 5% input increase on a 30% margin product costs a sixth of your profit if unpassed.

## FAQ

### What is a good gross margin?

It depends on the trade: 10–15% in grocery, 40–60% in apparel, 65–70% in food service. Compare with your category and with your own fixed costs.

### Is gross margin the same as markup?

No. Margin is profit as a share of price; markup is profit as a share of cost. A 50% markup is a 33.3% margin.

### Should GST be included in cost or price?

Neither, if you claim input credit and collect GST on sales. Use tax-exclusive figures. Composition dealers should treat the tax they pay as a cost.

### How do I price for a target margin?

Price = Cost ÷ (1 − target margin). For 40% on a ₹300 cost: 300 ÷ 0.6 = ₹500.

### Do shipping and packaging count in COGS?

Inward freight and packaging that goes with the product, yes. Outbound shipping is usually a variable selling cost below gross profit, though D2C brands often include it.
