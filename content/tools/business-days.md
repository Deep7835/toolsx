"Within 45 days", "30 working days", "7 business days after receipt" — contracts and statutes count days differently, and the difference decides whether a payment is late, a notice is valid or a delivery has missed its SLA. This calculator counts **working days between two dates** (excluding Sundays, optionally Saturdays or alternate Saturdays, national holidays and the festival holidays you pick), and also **adds N working days** to a start date to find a deadline. It runs in your browser and includes Indian national holidays for every year.

## Two modes

- **Days between**: enter a start and end date; get the number of working days, weekends and holidays in the range, inclusive or exclusive of the end date.
- **Add working days**: enter a start date and a count; get the resulting date, skipping non-working days. Useful for "deliver within 10 working days" or "reply within 15 working days".

Choose the weekend pattern: Sundays only (most shops), all Saturdays (offices, banks partially), or **second and fourth Saturdays** (banks and many government offices). Tick national holidays (Republic Day, Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti) and add your state or company holidays for festivals — Diwali, Holi, Eid, Pongal — which vary by year and state.

## Where the count matters

- **Section 43B(h) and the MSMED Act**: payments to micro and small suppliers are due within 45 days of acceptance (15 days without a written agreement) — calendar days, not working days. Interest at three times the RBI bank rate runs after that; compute it with the [late payment interest calculator](/tools/late-payment-interest) and read [Section 43B(h): the 45-day payment rule](/blog/section-43b-h-45-day-payment-rule).
- **GST**: due dates are calendar dates and are **not** extended for holidays; the [GST filing calendar](/tools/gst-calendar) shows them.
- **Cheque bounce notices** under Section 138: a demand notice within 30 days of the return memo, 15 days for payment, complaint within 30 days after that — calendar days. See [Cheque bounce: the 30-day notice, 15-day window](/blog/cheque-bounce-section-138-what-to-do).
- **Consumer refunds** under the e-commerce rules: "within a reasonable time", often stated as 7–10 working days in refund policies.
- **Bank strikes and holidays** disrupt clearing; the [bank strike business continuity plan](/blog/bank-strike-28-30-september-2026-keep-your-business-running) shows why counting bank working days matters for payroll.

## How to use the calculator

1. Pick the mode: between dates or add working days.
2. Enter the dates (or the start date and number of days).
3. Set the weekend pattern and holiday options; add custom holidays for your state.
4. Read the result — working days, and a list of the holidays that fell in the range so you can justify the count to a counterparty.
5. Copy the deadline into your contract, PO or notice.

## Drafting tips

- Say "calendar days" or "working days" explicitly in every contract clause; ambiguity defaults to calendar days in most interpretations.
- Define the weekend and the holiday list ("as per the RBI holiday list for Maharashtra") so both parties count the same way.
- For SLAs, state the time zone and the cut-off hour ("received before 5 pm IST counts as that day").
- Build buffers: a 10-working-day delivery promise in late October may span Diwali; check with the calculator before committing.

## FAQ

### Are Saturdays working days in India?

It depends on the establishment. Banks and many government offices close on the second and fourth Saturdays; most offices treat all Saturdays as off; shops usually work six days. The calculator lets you choose.

### Which holidays are included by default?

The three national holidays — 26 January, 15 August and 2 October. Festival and state holidays vary and can be added as custom dates.

### Is the 45-day MSME payment window in working days?

No, calendar days from the date of acceptance (or deemed acceptance) of goods or services.

### Does the end date count?

You choose. "Inclusive" counts both the start and end dates; "exclusive" counts days after the start up to and including the end.

### Can I count business days for a bank-related deadline?

Yes — set the weekend to second and fourth Saturdays and add the RBI holiday list for your state as custom holidays.
