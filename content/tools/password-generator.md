The GST portal, the bank, the marketplace seller account, the email that resets all of them, the WhatsApp Business API, the router — a small business runs on twenty passwords, and the one that is "Shop@2024" everywhere is the one that gets phished. This generator creates **strong, random passwords and passphrases** in your browser using the cryptographic random source of your device: choose length, character sets, or a memorable multi-word passphrase, and copy it into your password manager. Nothing is generated on a server; nothing is stored.

## What makes a password strong

Strength is about **length and randomness**, not clever substitutions. "P@ssw0rd!" is cracked in minutes by dictionary rules; "vault-cobra-monsoon-ledger-72" is not. Guidelines:

- **16+ characters** for anything that matters; 12 is the floor for low-risk accounts.
- **Random**, from a generator — not a name, a date or a pattern.
- **Unique per site**: a breach at one service must not open the others.
- **Passphrases** of four to six random words are as strong as complex strings and far easier to type on a phone.
- Add **two-factor authentication** wherever offered — the GST portal, bank, Google, Meta and marketplaces all support it.

Check any existing password's resistance to brute force with the [password strength checker](/tools/password-strength).

## How to use the generator

1. Choose **random string** or **passphrase**.
2. For strings, set the length (16–24 recommended) and which sets to include — upper, lower, digits, symbols; exclude look-alike characters (O/0, l/1) if it will be typed by hand.
3. For passphrases, choose the number of words (4–6), the separator and whether to add a number.
4. Copy the result; it is cleared from the screen when you leave the page.
5. Store it in a password manager (the browser's built-in one is fine for a start) — never in a WhatsApp note.

## Where small businesses get breached

- **GST and income-tax portal OTP fraud**: callers pretending to be officers ask for the OTP "to verify". No portal ever asks; see [GST scam calls, fake notices and OTP fraud](/blog/gst-scam-calls-and-fake-notices).
- **Shared passwords** with ex-staff still working — rotate when someone leaves, along with the [WiFi password](/tools/wifi-qr).
- **Reused email passwords**: the email that resets everything needs the strongest password and 2FA.
- **Website admin logins** on WordPress with default usernames — the hardening checklist is in [WordPress 7.1.1 security update](/blog/wordpress-7-1-1-click2shell-update-now-checklist).
- **Router defaults** (admin/admin) on the shop's broadband.

## Policy for a small team

- One password manager for the business, shared vaults per role.
- Unique 16+ character passwords for every service; passphrases for anything typed on a phone.
- 2FA mandatory on email, bank, GST, marketplaces and social accounts.
- Rotate shared credentials on staff exit — add it to the [experience letter](/tools/experience-letter) checklist.
- Never send passwords or OTPs over WhatsApp or SMS.

## FAQ

### Is the password generated on your server?

No. It is generated in your browser using the device's cryptographic random number generator and never transmitted.

### How long should a password be?

At least 16 characters for important accounts; passphrases of 4–6 random words are equally strong and easier to remember.

### Are passphrases really secure?

Yes — five random words from a large list have more entropy than a 12-character complex string, and are far less likely to be written on a sticky note.

### Should I change passwords regularly?

Only when there is reason — a breach, a departing employee, a shared device. Forced rotation without cause leads to weaker, patterned passwords.

### What if the site limits password length?

Use the maximum it allows with all character sets, and turn on 2FA for that account; report the limit to the provider.
