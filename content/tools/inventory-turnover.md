Stock on the shelf is cash that has stopped moving. Inventory turnover tells you how many times a year that cash comes back — cost of goods sold divided by average inventory — and its twin, days sales of inventory, tells you how many days a rupee sits in stock before a customer pays for it. Together they explain why a profitable shop can be permanently short of cash. This calculator computes **turnover ratio and DSI** for a product, a category or the whole business, and shows how much cash a faster turn would release.

## The formulas

- **Inventory turnover** = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average inventory (at cost), where average inventory = (opening + closing) ÷ 2.
- **Days sales of inventory (DSI)** = 365 ÷ Turnover.
- **Cash released** by improving turnover = COGS ÷ old turnover − COGS ÷ new turnover.

A garment shop with ₹72 lakh annual COGS and average stock of ₹18 lakh turns 4× a year; DSI is 91 days. Raising turnover to 6× (DSI 61) would free ₹6 lakh of cash — roughly the working-capital loan it currently pays 14% on.

## How to use the calculator

1. Enter COGS for the period (from your books, or sales × (1 − gross margin) using the [gross margin calculator](/tools/gross-margin)).
2. Enter opening and closing inventory at cost (or average inventory directly).
3. Choose the period (year, quarter, month); the tool annualises.
4. Read turnover, DSI, and the cash tied up; move the target-turnover slider to see the cash released.
5. Run it per category — the overall number hides the slow lines.

## Benchmarks (indicative, India)

| Trade | Turnover per year | DSI |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery / kirana | 12–24 | 15–30 days |
| Pharmacy | 6–10 | 35–60 |
| Apparel | 3–5 | 75–120 |
| Electronics retail | 6–10 | 35–60 |
| Furniture | 2–4 | 90–180 |
| Manufacturing (raw + finished) | 4–8 | 45–90 |

Low turnover is not always bad (jewellery, books) and high turnover can mean stock-outs. Read it with your gross margin: margin × turnover ≈ gross margin return on inventory, the number that decides which categories deserve shelf space.

## Speeding up the turn

- **Order smaller, more often**; the [purchase order](/tools/purchase-order) tool makes repeat orders quick.
- **Forecast by category** and check errors with the [forecast accuracy calculator](/tools/forecast-accuracy); over-forecasting is the usual root of dead stock.
- **Clear slow lines early**: a 20% markdown at 60 days beats 50% at 180; the [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) shows the margin after markdown.
- **Track by SKU** with barcodes ([barcode generator](/tools/barcode-generator)) so counts are real.
- **Consignment and sale-or-return** for seasonal items — goods move on a [delivery challan](/tools/delivery-challan) and are invoiced when sold.
- **Festive planning**: stock up 6–8 weeks before Diwali but plan the sell-through; the timeline is in the [festive season checklist](/blog/festive-season-2026-retail-checklist-diwali).

## Stock and tax

Closing stock is valued at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower; obsolete stock written down reduces taxable profit. GST input credit on unsold stock stays with you, but goods destroyed or written off require ITC reversal. Keep the stock register; it is also the base for the audit trail if turnover crosses the tax-audit threshold.

## FAQ

### What is a good inventory turnover ratio?

Grocery 12–24×, apparel 3–5×, electronics 6–10×. Compare with your category and your own past periods; a rising number with stable stock-outs is the goal.

### Should I use sales or COGS?

COGS, and inventory at cost, so both sides are on the same basis. Using sales overstates turnover by your margin.

### How do I get average inventory?

(Opening stock + closing stock) ÷ 2 for the period. For seasonal businesses, average monthly stock levels for a truer figure.

### What does a high DSI mean?

Money is sitting in stock for a long time — usually over-ordering, slow lines, or too many SKUs. Break it down by category to find where.

### Does GST affect the calculation?

Use stock and COGS excluding creditable GST. Composition dealers, who cannot claim ITC, include the tax in cost.
