State Bank of India issued a customer advisory this weekend: the **United Forum of Bank Unions** has called a nationwide strike from **Monday 28 to Wednesday 30 September 2026**. Because **26 September is a fourth Saturday** and **27 September a Sunday**, branch banking could be disrupted for **five consecutive days**. The Finance Ministry meets bank chiefs on Monday 21st to plan contingencies, and strikes are sometimes called off at the last minute — but plan as if it happens.

## What will and won't work

| Service | During the strike |
|---|---|
| UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS via net/mobile banking | **Working** — these are automated |
| ATMs | Working, but cash replenishment may lag by the third day |
| Debit/credit cards, POS | Working |
| Branch cash deposit / withdrawal | **Disrupted** at public-sector banks; private banks largely open |
| Cheque clearing (CTS) | **Delayed** — clearing houses depend on PSB staff |
| Demand drafts, locker access, KYC, account opening | Disrupted at PSBs |
| Loan disbursals, LC/BG issuance, forex | Disrupted at PSBs |
| Government payment gateways (GST, income tax) | Working — net banking/UPI |

Private banks (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak) and small finance banks are generally not part of UFBU strikes, though some old-generation private banks are.

## Do this by Friday 25 September

### Payroll
Wages under the Code on Wages must be paid by the **7th of the following month**, so September salaries are not legally at risk. But if you pay on the 30th or 1st, **initiate NEFT/IMPS batches on Friday** or schedule them in net banking. Cash-paid staff: withdraw the wage float before Friday. Compute the roster with the [team payroll calculator](/tools/team-payroll).

### Cheques
Any cheque you deposit on Friday may clear only on 1–2 October. Ask customers who pay by cheque to pay by UPI/NEFT this week instead; send them the [payment receipt](/tools/payment-receipt) with a UPI QR for the exact amount. Don't issue post-dated cheques falling due on the 28th–30th; bounced-cheque timelines don't pause for strikes.

### Cash float
Retailers: keep two to three days of change and petty cash. ATMs run dry on day two or three of a strike in smaller towns.

### Loans and EMIs
Auto-debits (NACH) run as scheduled — ensure balances. If a loan disbursal or LC is pending at a PSB, push for release before the 25th.

### GST and tax
No due dates fall in the strike window (GSTR-3B for August was due 20 September; the next is GSTR-1 on 11 October). Advance tax's second instalment was 15 September. Nothing to do — but check the [GST calendar](/tools/gst-calendar) if you are on a special schedule.

### Government collections
Customs, GST refunds and government payments routed through PSBs may slip a few days.

## The week is also holiday-heavy

Several states have regional bank holidays between 21 and 27 September on top of the weekend. Count working days for any deadline with the [business days calculator](/tools/business-days) and add your state's holidays.

## Why the strike

Unions are pressing long-standing demands — a five-day banking week, adequate recruitment against vacancies, and pension updation. A strike was averted in 2025 after talks; the Ministry's Monday meeting is aimed at the same outcome. Watch SBI's and your bank's advisories on the 22nd–24th.

## FAQ

### Will UPI work during the bank strike?
Yes. UPI, IMPS and NEFT are automated and unaffected. Only staff-dependent branch services are hit.

### Are private banks on strike?
Most new-generation private banks are not part of UFBU. A few old private-sector banks may join. Check your bank's advisory.

### Will my salary be delayed?
Only if your employer pays in cash or by cheque through a PSB branch. Electronic salary credits go through.

### What if the strike is called off?
Nothing you did above is wasted — earlier salary batches and UPI collection are simply good practice.
